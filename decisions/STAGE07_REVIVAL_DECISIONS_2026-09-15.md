## D7-1 — Welfare interpretation

The repaired B-FIX/FULL coalition-threshold result is retained as economically meaningful. At `(v,gamma)=(.11,.10)`, the FULL SU national-welfare gain over IS is positive and decomposes into a dominant domestic-profit gain plus a smaller positive consumer-surplus contribution.

## D7-4 — Institutional status of standards depth

`depth -> realized interoperability` is institutionally defensible when standards have multiple functions/options/interfaces and interoperability depends on implementation/profile/test coverage.

The linear map `chi(s)=s/s_bar` remains a reduced-form normalization. No real standards body is claimed to choose the literal scalar `s`.

ETSI testing/conformance architecture and SAE J3400 are retained as motivating examples, not calibration evidence.

## D7-5 — Generality boundary

Essential economic ingredients:

1. intensive standards/interoperability choice;
2. compatibility gain from depth;
3. competitive compression from harmonization;
4. separate costly post-policy design margin;
5. national objectives combining domestic consumer surplus and domestic producer profit;
6. selection-safe continuation.

Model-specific objects not authorized as general results include three-country symmetry, quadratic demand, circular/cosine product space, linear interoperability realization, equal populations, the exact cap, and the numerical threshold values.

## D7-6 — Low-gamma multiplicity

The low-redesign-cost crossing equilibrium remains a genuine model limitation and permanent regression. It may be used as a scope boundary or appendix qualification, not as evidence of robustness.
