## D-6R2-05 — Strongest closest-paper threat

**Decision:** The strongest single strategic-timing threat remains **Ruiz (2004)** because it already has government standards recognition → endogenous product characteristics → price competition → national welfare and excessive strategic differentiation.

The strongest institutional/network threat remains **Gandal & Shy (2001)**.

The strongest composite attack is therefore **Ruiz (2004) + Gandal–Shy (2001)**.

This composite is classified `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE`, not `NEAR-ABSORBS`, because the C-ESD national IS/SU ranking reversal is not an immediate corollary of their existing propositions. Ruiz itself reports qualitative robustness of the policy equilibrium after product characteristics become endogenous.

## D-6R2-06 — Strongest post-Stage-4R repaired-instrument prior art

**Decision:** Add **Takarada, Kawabata, Yanase & Kurata (2020), “Standards Policy and International Trade: Multilateralism versus Regionalism”** as a top-tier closest paper.

It already contains three countries, national/regional/multilateral standards regimes, a jointly selected common regional standard, national welfare, and core/blocking; only a regional standards agreement can be in the core in some parameter regions.

Also record **Kawabata & Takarada (2021)** and **Stadler, Trexler & Unsorg (2022)** as important common-standard/intensity priors.

Implication: Stage-4R's coalition-level depth construction is not novelty and must not be marketed as such.
