## 1. Audited candidate object

Stage 2 does not evaluate the generic claim that standards or compatibility affect firm behavior. That claim is already occupied. The audited residual object from Stage 1 is the whole-game question:

`standards partition -> endogenous bloc standards depth -> costly post-policy product repositioning -> Bertrand competition -> national welfare -> coalition blocking/stability`.

The source model itself does **not** currently deliver the desired coalition-level reversal. The Stage-2 question is whether an absorption/non-absorption theorem or a repositioning-essential stability result is already known or structurally contained in prior work.

## 3. Own-research internal prior art

### 3.1 `private-compatibility-standards-coalitions`

This project already contains the broad architecture

`formal government partition -> private standards adoption -> competition -> national welfare -> coalition stability`.

Therefore the revival cannot claim novelty from actor separation, post-partition private response, national welfare, or standards-coalition stability as such.

Relationship: `OWN NESTED BENCHMARK / COMPONENT OVERLAP`.

### 3.2 `standardization-scope-direction-innovation`

Paper: **“Standardization Scope and Endogenous Innovation Portfolios.”**

This project already contains

`regulator-chosen continuous standardization scope -> firms reallocate fixed R&D capacity between common and proprietary innovation -> Bertrand prices -> welfare -> optimal selective vs complete standardization`.

Therefore the revival cannot claim novelty from the generic statement that a continuous standardization policy changes firms' downstream strategic choices and thereby reverses a policy ranking. SSDI's downstream margin is innovation portfolio composition rather than product positioning, and it has no multilateral standards-coalition stability layer.

Relationship: `OWN STRUCTURALLY CLOSE BENCHMARK`.

## 4. Component-level claims killed at Stage 2

The following claims are prohibited as headline novelty claims:

1. standards / compatibility affect downstream competition;
2. compatibility chosen before product design induces stronger differentiation;
3. endogenous product differentiation can change compatibility incentives;
4. coalition structure chosen first changes later product quality/location and prices;
5. government standards policy can affect endogenous product characteristics;
6. technical compatibility can be a continuous policy variable;
7. regional and multilateral standards agreements can have different coalition-stability properties;
8. costly product repositioning around inherited positions affects prices and welfare;
9. interoperability coalition membership can change a later endogenous investment/resource decision and coalition size;
10. continuous standardization policy can alter a downstream strategic portfolio choice and thereby change the regulator's optimal policy (already present in the author's SSDI project);
11. coalitional interoperability configurations can produce different price/welfare outcomes.

## 5. Whole-game absorption test

### 5.1 Is there one prior model reproducing the full revival game after relabeling?

**No identified paper does so.**

The closest pieces are distributed across different models:

- Gandal–Shy / Takarada: government standards coalitions and stability;
- Klimenko: continuous government compatibility policy;
- Woeckener / Ruiz: post-policy endogenous product differentiation/design;
- Economides–Skrzypacz / Huang–Tan–Teh–Zhou: standards/interoperability coalition structure and downstream competition;
- Guo–Liu–Nault: interoperability coalition formation with a post-coalition endogenous resource choice;
- SSDI: continuous standardization policy causing a downstream strategic reallocation and a policy-ranking reversal.

Reconstructing the revival still requires combining different players, objectives, timing, and strategic margins. That component overlap is not by itself exact prior art under workflow v2.1.

### 5.2 Is the desired headline result an immediate corollary of an existing theorem?

**Not identified.**

No located theorem states conditions under which an endogenous standards-depth instrument absorbs versus fails to absorb post-standard product repositioning, or establishes that allowing repositioning changes the stable standards-coalition set after every bloc optimally chooses its own depth.

This is a surviving **candidate result class**, not a proven novelty claim.

## 6. Strongest 2024–2026 threats

### Guo, Liu & Nault (2024)

This paper is materially closer than older literature lists because coalition participation changes interoperability and the coalition then makes an endogenous resource-investment decision. It demonstrates that `interoperability coalition -> endogenous post-coalition action -> equilibrium coalition size` is itself not new. The revival must therefore make the **product-repositioning / policy-depth interaction** indispensable, not merely append a downstream choice to a coalition game.

### Huang, Tan, Teh & Zhou (2026)

This contemporary platform paper parameterizes interoperability strength and configuration, including coalition-based structures, and derives pricing/welfare effects. It substantially narrows any claim based on the structure or strength of interoperability. The revival must not sell coalitional interoperability, price effects, or welfare rankings as novel independently of endogenous repositioning and endogenous government coalition stability.

### SSDI internal threat

The author's SSDI paper already demonstrates a continuous standardization policy changing an endogenous downstream firm allocation and reversing the policy ranking. This makes the revival's own contribution test stricter: the relevant new object must be the **coalition-formation/stability consequence of product repositioning**, or an absorption/non-absorption theorem specific to that game.

## 7. Surviving novelty candidate

The narrow surviving candidate is:

> In a multilateral technical-standards coalition game where each standards bloc endogenously chooses harmonization depth before firms incur costly product repositioning and then compete, characterize when bloc policy adjustment fully absorbs the downstream repositioning channel and when it cannot; in the non-absorption region, establish that the stable coalition set or a stability threshold differs from the otherwise identical fixed-position benchmark.

This survives Stage 2 only as a **whole-game / theorem-level candidate**.

It is not enough to show:

- nonzero repositioning;
- a fixed-depth welfare reversal;
- a different optimal standards depth;
- a small welfare-level change;
- compatibility becoming more attractive after differentiation.

## 8. Required nested benchmarks for Stage 3/4

Any selected architecture must preserve explicit comparison against:

- `B-FIX`: identical standards coalition/depth game with product locations fixed;
- `B-EXO`: product repositioning with exogenous or zero policy depth;
- `FULL`: endogenous coalition/depth + endogenous repositioning;
- SSDI as an external-to-model conceptual benchmark showing continuous standardization -> endogenous firm response -> policy reversal without coalition stability;
- Guo–Liu–Nault as an external conceptual benchmark showing interoperability coalition -> endogenous resource decision -> coalition-size equilibrium.

The main theorem must be unavailable in `B-FIX` and `B-EXO` separately.

## 9. Stage-2 implication for mechanism search

Stage 3 may search for a minimal mechanism that breaks the Stage-1 policy-adjustment absorption result, but only if the new primitive has an independent economic rationale.

The preferred candidate cannot be chosen at Stage 2. Stage 3 must compare alternatives and reject any modification that merely forces `s_I>0` or directly builds in the desired coalition reversal.
