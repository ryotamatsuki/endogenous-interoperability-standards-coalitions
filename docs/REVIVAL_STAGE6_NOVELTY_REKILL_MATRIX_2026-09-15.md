## 2. Proposition-level novelty objects

### N1 — realized-interoperability depth trade-off

Deeper standards integration simultaneously raises realized interoperability and compresses a source of differentiation/raises competitive pressure.

**Novelty status: KILLED AS A STANDALONE CLAIM.**

This trade-off is structurally occupied by compatibility/network literature, especially Economides–Skrzypacz, Klimenko, and contemporary interoperability work. The 2026 Menegaki–Serfes breadth/depth paper also studies coordination depth as a stability/competition margin.

### N2 — compatibility/standards policy followed by endogenous product differentiation

Compatibility or standards policy changes later product design/location, which can soften competition.

**Novelty status: KILLED AS A STANDALONE CLAIM.**

Woeckener (1999) directly contains compatibility first -> product differentiation later -> softer location competition. Ruiz (2004) also allows endogenous product characteristics under government standard-recognition policy. Gabszewicz–Marini–Tarola (2015) contains coalition first -> endogenous quality -> prices in a non-standards alliance setting.

### N3 — coalition/interoperability arrangement followed by another endogenous strategic action

Coalition membership or interoperability changes a subsequent investment/resource/competitive decision.

**Novelty status: KILLED AS A STANDALONE CLAIM.**

Guo–Liu–Nault (2024) explicitly models coalition participation, differential interoperability, and subsequent cooperative resource investment. The author's SSDI project already contains continuous standardization policy -> endogenous downstream strategic reallocation -> welfare/policy reversal.

### N4 — standards/coordination breadth or depth changes coalition stability

Changing the scope/depth of coordination changes which coalition is stable.

**Novelty status: KILLED AS A STANDALONE CLAIM.**

Takarada (2020) studies regional versus multilateral standards stability. Economides–Skrzypacz studies standards-coalition size as network effects change. Menegaki–Serfes (2026), *Breadth, Depth, and Coalition Stability in Complementary Oligopoly*, explicitly characterizes stable coordination architectures along breadth and depth margins. Their separate current working-paper summary, *The Architecture of Voluntary Standards in Complementary Oligopoly*, explicitly frames a voluntary interoperability standard in terms of participation breadth/depth and a private-versus-welfare preferred depth wedge.

### N5 — costly post-policy repositioning shifts a government standards-coalition blocking threshold after endogenous depth optimization

Compare the same government standards partition/depth game with and without endogenous product repositioning. In the repaired higher-`gamma` region, the SU-vs-IS blocking threshold differs, and the stable set can differ at the same primitives.

**Novelty status: SURVIVES — POTENTIALLY NOVEL / RESULT-LEVEL.**

No located paper states or implies this result as an immediate corollary. The closest literatures each omit at least one proof-critical interaction:

- Woeckener: compatibility + later product differentiation, but no government coalition/depth/stability problem;
- Takarada: government standards coalition/core stability, but no post-policy product repositioning;
- Klimenko: continuous government compatibility policy, but no multilateral coalition blocking with costly repositioning;
- Economides–Skrzypacz: standards coalition + network/competition trade-off, but no separate endogenous depth followed by costly product repositioning and national-welfare blocking;
- Guo–Liu–Nault: interoperability coalition + endogenous resource decision + coalition size, but no differentiated-product repositioning/price competition/government standards-depth game;
- Menegaki–Serfes 2026 breadth/depth work: coalition stability and depth architecture, but in complementary oligopoly and without post-standard costly product repositioning shifting a government standards-coalition blocking threshold;
- own SSDI: standardization scope + endogenous innovation portfolio + welfare, but no multilateral standards coalition blocking/stability and no product repositioning.

## 4. Whole-game absorption test after certification

### Can one prior model reproduce FULL by relabeling?

**No identified model can.**

The closest direct decompositions remain:

- government standards stability: Takarada;
- compatibility-depth policy: Klimenko;
- compatibility-before-product-design: Woeckener/Ruiz;
- standards coalition/network competition: Economides–Skrzypacz;
- interoperability coalition/post-coalition action: Guo–Liu–Nault;
- coalition breadth/depth stability: Menegaki–Serfes;
- costly repositioning: Kishihara–Matsubayashi.

No one of them contains the full strategic-feedback network where government bloc depth changes realized interoperability and competitive pressure, firms then pay to reposition on a distinct horizontal characteristic, national welfare feeds back into strict government coalition blocking, and the repositioning margin shifts the blocking threshold relative to a fixed-position policy game.

### Is N5 an immediate corollary of a prior theorem?

**No identified theorem makes N5 immediate.**

Woeckener establishes that compatibility commitment can become more attractive because later differentiation softens location competition, but does not map that effect into an optimized multilateral government standards-coalition blocking threshold. Menegaki–Serfes establishes that coordination depth can determine coalition stability, but the available 2026 models do not use costly post-standard product repositioning as the reason the fixed-position and endogenous-position blocking thresholds differ.
