## 1. Certified object carried into Stage 7

The only surviving Stage-6 novelty claim is the repaired higher-redesign-cost result:

> After standards blocs optimize realized-interoperability depth, costly post-policy product repositioning can shift the bilateral-union versus international-standardization national-welfare blocking threshold relative to the otherwise identical fixed-position policy game, thereby changing the stable standards partition.

Stage 7 does not revive any killed setup-level novelty claim and does not use the low-`gamma` multiplicity region as a selection-free theorem.

The repaired interpretation point is `(v,gamma)=(.11,.10)` with full-depth IS/SU/SW policy continuations as certified by the repeat Stage 4A attack.

## 2. Welfare accounting

National welfare remains

`W_i = CS/3 + Pi_i`,

where equal national consumer populations split common consumer surplus equally and each country owns its domestic firm. Foreign-firm profit is excluded.

This accounting is an explicit model convention and is not labeled a first-best or global social-welfare criterion.

### 2.1 Repaired witness decomposition

At `(v,gamma)=(.11,.10)`:

| Object | Domestic profit | Domestic CS share | National welfare |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| IS | 0.09322289 | 0.05005937 | 0.14328226 |
| SU, fixed positions | 0.09283986 | 0.05006938 | 0.14290923 |
| SU, FULL repositioning | 0.09329374 | 0.05007073 | 0.14336446 |

Hence:

- `FULL SU - IS`: profit `+0.00007085`, CS share `+0.00001136`, national welfare `+0.00008220`;
- `B-FIX SU - IS`: profit `-0.00038303`, CS share `+0.00001001`, national welfare `-0.00037303`;
- `FULL SU - B-FIX SU`: profit `+0.00045388`, CS share `+0.00000135`, national welfare `+0.00045523`.

The coalition-ranking reversal is therefore mainly a domestic-producer-rent mechanism. At the repaired witness, roughly 86% of the FULL-SU national-welfare gain over IS is the domestic-profit component and roughly 14% is the domestic consumer-surplus component.

This matters for interpretation: the paper should not sell the result as a broad consumer-welfare gain from regional standardization. The central result is organizational/coalitional: product repositioning can change the national incentive to form a standards union because firms can restore some differentiation after harmonization.

## 3. Firm-profit versus national-welfare wedge

At `gamma=.10`, Stage-7 diagnostics give two distinct FULL indifference thresholds:

- domestic-firm profit threshold: `v_private approximately .11805780`;
- national-welfare threshold: `v_national approximately .11964007`.

Thus there is a narrow interval

`.11805780 < v < .11964007`

in which the domestic firm prefers IS to SU but the national government still prefers SU because the consumer-surplus contribution offsets the private-profit ranking.

This is a secondary welfare result, not the headline novelty claim. It shows that the government blocking problem is not exactly a firm-profit coalition game.

In the fixed-position benchmark, the profit and national-welfare indifference thresholds coincide at `1/15`, so the small private/national wedge itself is generated by the endogenous positioning continuation.

## 4. Economic meaning of the threshold shift

At `gamma=.10`:

- B-FIX blocking threshold: `v_FIX=1/15 approximately .0666667`;
- FULL blocking threshold: `v_FULL approximately .1196401`.

The threshold shift is approximately `.05297`, or about 79% of the B-FIX threshold.

The level welfare difference at one witness is small, but coalition formation is discrete. Therefore the economically relevant object is not the absolute welfare level alone: it is the parameter interval over which a regional standards union can strictly block international standardization.

Interpreting `v` as the value of realized interoperability, stronger interoperability eventually favors IS because IS realizes compatibility across all three country pairs whereas a bilateral SU only internalizes the member link. Product repositioning raises the `v` level up to which the bilateral union remains nationally attractive.

Numerical Stage-7 diagnostics within the repeat-Stage-4A higher-`gamma` stress range give:

| .08 | .12136385 | .12309062 |

These are finite numerical sensitivity results, not a continuous comparative-statics theorem.

The pattern is economically coherent: within the selection-safe high-cost region, lower redesign cost allows more repositioning and sustains the SU ranking up to a larger interoperability value. At sufficiently low redesign cost, however, the permanent Stage-4A crossing-equilibrium counterexample reappears, so the paper must not extrapolate this monotonic pattern globally.

## 6. Institutional validation of `depth`

The C1 interpretation requires interoperability to have an intensive margin rather than being purely binary. Current standards practice gives support for that reduced-form idea, although not for the literal linear function `chi(s)=s/s_bar`.

### 6.1 ETSI testing architecture

ETSI distinguishes conformance from interoperability testing. Its conformance framework uses an Implementation Conformance Statement (ICS), described as a checklist of the capabilities/features/options implemented by a product, and uses those capabilities to select and parameterize test cases. ETSI's interoperability framework uses an Interoperable Functions Statement (IFS), identifying functions supported by the equipment and testing selected functional requirements end-to-end.

Primary sources:

- ETSI, **Conformance Testing**: https://portal.etsi.org/Services/Centre-for-Testing-Interoperability/ETSI-Approach/Conformance
- ETSI, **Interoperability Testing**: https://portal.etsi.org/Services/Centre-for-Testing-Interoperability/ETSI-Approach/Interoperability-Testing
- ETSI, **Testing and interoperability**: https://www.etsi.org/technologies/testing-interoperability/

This supports the paper's qualitative interpretation that a common standards architecture can realize different amounts of functional interoperability depending on supported options/functions, profiles, and validated interfaces.

### 6.2 EV charging as an application example

SAE J3400 is not merely a connector-shape label. The published scope covers physical, electrical, functional, safety, and performance requirements, and the U.S. Joint Office describes standardized communication protocols as important to compatibility and interoperability between charging stations and EVs. The Recommended Practice is part of an evolving architecture with additional features/functions and interoperability.

Primary/official sources:

- SAE J3400_202409 scope: https://saemobilus.sae.org/standards/j3400_202409-north-american-charging-system-nacs-electric-vehicles
- U.S. Joint Office, **SAE J3400 Charging Connector**: https://driveelectric.gov/charging-connector
- U.S. Joint Office, **J3400 Recommended Practice**: https://driveelectric.gov/news/j3400-recommended-practice

This is a suitable motivating example for standards depth, but the paper must not claim that SAE or ETSI literally choose the scalar `s` in the model.

## 7. Institutional conclusion

**PASS WITH QUALIFICATION.**

The primitive `depth -> realized interoperability` has defensible institutional content in environments where standards specify multiple functions/options/interfaces and where implementation/testing determines how much end-to-end interoperability is actually realized.

The linear map `chi(s)=s/s_bar` is a tractability normalization. It should be presented as a reduced form for implementation breadth/completeness or interoperability realization, not as a direct measurement equation for any one standard.

## 8. Generality boundary

The mechanism should be stated in economic rather than industry-specific language.

Essential ingredients are:

1. a standards coalition can choose an intensive interoperability/harmonization margin;
2. deeper harmonization increases realized compatibility/interoperability;
3. deeper harmonization also compresses one dimension of product differentiation/competitive distance;
4. firms retain a separate costly design/location margin after standards policy;
5. coalition governments value domestic consumer surplus plus domestic producer profit;
6. the relevant continuation is in the higher-redesign-cost selection-safe class.

The following are tractability/normalization choices and should not be sold as general primitives:

- three symmetric countries;
- circular horizontal characteristics;
- cosine proximity `phi`;
- quadratic representative-consumer demand;
- linear realization `chi`;
- equal consumer populations;
- exact cap `s_bar=.25`;
- the particular numerical thresholds.

The paper has not yet proved the threshold-shift result for arbitrary demand systems, arbitrary realization functions, asymmetric countries, or arbitrary redesign-cost functions. Those stronger claims are prohibited pending Stage 7.5/7.5A.

## 9. Testable predictions

The certified mechanism yields the following empirical/institutional predictions, stated as mechanism predictions rather than estimated facts:

1. **Re-differentiation after deeper harmonization.** When a technical standard compresses differentiation on standardized attributes, participating firms should increase differentiation on remaining nonstandardized attributes when redesign is feasible.
2. **Coalition viability depends on redesign flexibility.** Within the selection-safe range, lower product-redesign costs should make a regional standards bloc viable over a wider range of interoperability benefits because member firms can offset competitive compression more strongly.
3. **Very low redesign costs may create coordination/multiplicity problems.** The model predicts that sufficiently flexible redesign can produce multiple product-position equilibria rather than a single smooth response.
4. **Consumer and producer rankings need not coincide.** Around the coalition-indifference threshold, a government can prefer an SU even after the domestic firm has switched to preferring IS, because consumer surplus contributes separately to national welfare.
5. **Fixed-product policy evaluation can misclassify coalition incentives.** Policy analyses that hold product characteristics fixed can understate the parameter range over which a regional standards union is nationally attractive.

## 11. Stage-7 verdict

**GO — GO TO STAGE 7.5 FULL-THEORY FREEZE DECISION.**

Why GO:

- the welfare result is not a pure accounting artifact;
- the headline stability-threshold shift is economically larger than the local welfare-level difference suggests;
- the institutional interpretation of an intensive interoperability margin is defensible;
- the mechanism yields falsifiable predictions;
- the result can be stated without relying on EV charging, telecom, or another single industry.

Why scope remains narrow:

- the welfare gain is primarily producer-rent driven;
- the private/national wedge is small and secondary;
- global generality beyond the certified high-`gamma` class is not proved;
- the linear interoperability-realization function is a reduced-form normalization;
- low-redesign-cost multiplicity is a real model limitation, not a robustness success.
