## 3. Mandatory-paper evidence status

### Gandal & Shy (2001)

- Journal: *Journal of International Economics* 53(2), 363–383.
- DOI: `10.1016/S0022-1996(00)00067-2`.
- Publisher page inspected: ScienceDirect.
- Institutional metadata inspected: Tel Aviv University.
- Accessible working-paper record inspected: AgEcon Search / RePEc.
- A direct PDF fetch from AgEcon Search returned access failure in the browsing environment; the indexed PDF text and publisher full article page exposed the model architecture and core results needed for the audit.
- Result confirmed: three-country standardization unions, network effects/conversion costs, participation incentives, possible two-country union; no post-policy product-characteristic/location choice.

### Ruiz (2004)

- Author-uploaded full-text working paper inspected through ResearchGate.
- Full text directly exposes Introduction, timing, endogenous-location section, propositions and conclusion.
- Result confirmed: governments choose recognition; firms can choose two-dimensional product characteristics after policy and before prices; national welfare includes domestic CS and domestic firm's home/foreign profits; endogenous differentiation can be excessive; qualitative government-policy equilibrium remains robust.
- This is the strongest post-policy strategic-response threat.

### Klimenko (2009)

- Journal: *Journal of International Economics* 77(2), 151–166.
- DOI: `10.1016/j.jinteco.2008.08.005`.
- Publisher article/extended abstract inspected; Georgia Tech institutional page and RePEc cross-checked.
- Publisher full PDF restricted.
- Result confirmed: continuous government compatibility policy, network effects, strategic policy, international agreements; no endogenous product-positioning stage or regional coalition stability.

### Matutes & Padilla (1994)

- Publisher abstract inspected (European Economic Review / ScienceDirect), DOI `10.1016/0014-2921(94)90040-X`.
- Result confirmed: ATM sharing among competing banks; equilibrium strict-subset sharing or incompatibility; no government standards coalition/product repositioning.

### Economides & Flyer (1997/98)

- NYU/author working-paper copy and SSRN metadata inspected.
- Result confirmed: compatibility/network benefits versus horizontal-differentiation/competition tradeoff.

### Economides & Skrzypacz (2003/04)

- NYU/SSRN/repository evidence inspected.
- Result confirmed: standards coalitions formed by firms, then oligopoly; coalition size trades network gains against intensified competition.

### Baake & Boom (2001)

- Publisher article inspected, DOI `10.1016/S0167-7187(99)00029-6`.
- Four-stage timing confirmed: inherent quality first, then compatibility/adapters, then prices, then consumers. This is not the C-ESD policy-before-repositioning timing.

### Jonard & Schenk (1999/2003)

- UNU/Maastricht institutional record and author-uploaded text inspected.
- Result confirmed: firms choose compatibility then prices; compatibility value versus loss of differentiation.

### Wang & Lyu (2020)

- Publisher abstract inspected, DOI `10.1016/j.ijpe.2020.107779`.
- Result confirmed: sequential endogenous product positioning under network effects; no government standards coalition.

### Ding, Ko & Shen (2022)

- Publisher full article page inspected, DOI `10.1016/j.econmod.2022.105989`.
- Result confirmed: three-platform compatibility choice; partial compatibility can be unique coalition-proof market outcome; welfare comparisons; no government/post-policy repositioning.

## 4. Newly emphasized / newly discovered Stage-6R2 papers

### Takarada, Kawabata, Yanase & Kurata (2020)

**Newly elevated to a top-tier closest paper after the Stage-4R repair.**

- Title: *Standards Policy and International Trade: Multilateralism versus Regionalism*.
- Journal: *Journal of Public Economic Theory* 22(5), 1420–1441.
- DOI: `10.1111/jpet.12425`.
- Wiley full article text was indexed and inspected in detail.
- Basic model: three symmetric countries; first-stage endogenous standards; second-stage firms choose quantities.
- Regional harmonization: two member governments choose a common regional standard to maximize joint welfare; outsider keeps its national standard.
- Multilateral harmonization: all three choose a common standard to maximize world welfare.
- Stability: core/blocking among countries.
- Main result: world-welfare-maximizing multilateral agreement is not always in the core; for sufficiently transboundary externalities, only a regional agreement can be in the core.
- Relevance: directly kills setup novelty of regional-vs-multilateral standards agreements, coalition-level common standard, and core stability. Missing C-ESD's post-policy horizontal product repositioning/network graph.

### Kawabata & Takarada (2021)

**Newly elevated as repaired-instrument prior art.**

- Title: *Deep Trade Agreements and Harmonization of Standards*.
- Journal: *Southern Economic Journal* 88(1), 118–143.
- DOI: `10.1002/soej.12500`.
- Wiley full article/abstract and author-uploaded copy discovered.
- Three-country oligopoly; RTA/CU members jointly set common standards; multilateral international standard; endogenous standards/tariffs.
- Deep FTA can be a stumbling block to multilateral harmonization/free trade.
- Missing product repositioning/network-coalition feedback.

### Stadler, Trexler & Unsorg (2022)

**Newly emphasized as a continuous common-compatibility-intensity analogue.**

- Title: *The Perpetual Trouble with Network Products Why IT Firms Choose Partial Compatibility*.
- Springer open-access article inspected, DOI `10.1007/s11067-022-09572-x`.
- Firms jointly choose common degree of compatibility/standardization before prices; partial compatibility can be SPNE.
- This is close to the repaired common-intensity object, but it is a firm-side two-firm standards/SDO game without government coalition/national welfare/product positioning.

### Peitz & Sato (2026)

- Title: *Asymmetric Platform Oligopoly*.
- *RAND Journal of Economics* 57(1), 60–77, DOI `10.1111/1756-2171.70008`.
- Current Wiley text inspected.
- Continuous degree of compatibility, platform network effects and price competition; DMA application; no coalition formation or endogenous product repositioning.

### Li, Sun, Song & Wu (2026)

- Title: *From Centrality Discounts to Centrality Premia: Interoperability and Platform Competition in Social Networks*.
- arXiv:2607.09269 (July 2026).
- Interoperability changes network-position pricing and can reverse centrality discounts into premia; no government coalition or post-policy product positioning.

## 7. False positives / excluded classes

Searches generated many non-absorbing items, especially:

- engineering/technical interoperability protocols without strategic economic choice;
- military/public-sector interoperability;
- legal descriptions of DMA interoperability without an equilibrium model;
- marketing uses of “standardization” unrelated to technical standards;
- standards/quality papers with no compatibility/interoperability meaning;
- interoperability papers with price effects but no coalition/stability object;
- coalition papers with no standards/interoperability policy;
- product-positioning papers with no government policy or coalition.

These were excluded from the closest-paper matrix unless they materially affected a killed broad claim.
