# Revival Stage 3 — Selected Mechanism for Stage 4

Date: 2026-09-10

Status: **SELECTED FOR MINIMAL-MODEL KILL TEST ONLY — NOT A THEORY FREEZE**

Selected mechanism: **C1 — Realized Interoperability Depth**.

## 1. Research question carried into Stage 4

> When formal standards coalitions choose technical depth before firms reposition products, can the fact that depth determines both realized interoperability and the competitive pressure of standardization make strategic repositioning consequential for the stable coalition outcome after policy optimization?

The objective is not to make a bilateral union win. Any stable-partition result is acceptable. The mechanism fails if endogenous repositioning does not change a coalition-level implication relative to the fixed-position benchmark.

## 2. Economic interpretation

PR #65 separates formal coalition membership from continuous depth in a problematic way: coalition membership activates the full compatibility/network term, while depth changes only `Tau` and therefore only the competitive/substitutability side. Stage 1 proved that this structure makes IS welfare fall with depth on the symmetric continuation and yields `s_I*=0` structurally.

The selected mechanism gives the existing depth variable its literal technical meaning:

- formal membership determines **who can interoperate under one common standard**;
- depth determines **how completely that interoperability is realized**;
- the same depth also reduces within-bloc standard-based differentiation / adaptation friction.

Thus deeper standardization has a benefit and a competition cost through one policy object. No second policy instrument, new player, market, bargaining stage, or heterogeneity parameter is added.

## 3. Minimal full game

Retain three countries, one domestic firm per country, equal national consumer populations, inherited product anchors, costly horizontal product repositioning, and affine-demand Bertrand competition.

Timing:

`rho -> bloc depths s -> product positions x -> prices p -> welfare -> coalition blocking/stability`.

### Stage G — standards partition

`rho in {IS, SU_12, SU_13, SU_23, SW}`.

### Stage P — bloc depth

Each bloc `C` uses the source policy domain `s_C in [0,s_bar]` and maximizes the sum of national welfare of its members. The source pairwise friction map is retained for the Stage-4 candidate:

`Tau_ij = t_bar-s_C` if `i,j` are in the same bloc `C`,

`Tau_ij = t_bar+(s_C+s_D)/2` if `i` and `j` belong to different blocs `C,D`.

The later source convention allowing singleton specificity depth is retained at the minimal-model gate so that C1 changes only one conceptual object.

### New C1 object — realized interoperability

Normalize the simplest baseline realization map as

`chi(s_C)=s_C/s_bar` for `s_bar>0`,

so `chi in [0,1]`.

For an off-diagonal pair,

`M_ij(rho,s)=chi(s_C)` if `i,j` belong to the same multi-country bloc `C`,

and

`M_ij(rho,s)=0` if they belong to different blocs.

This means a zero-depth formal agreement delivers no realized cross-firm interoperability, while a full-depth agreement delivers the full within-bloc compatibility intensity. `chi` is fixed before any Stage-4 parameter/result search. Nonlinear `chi` is prohibited in the Stage-4 baseline.

### Stage X — firm repositioning

Firm `i` chooses `x_i` on the unit product-characteristic circle and pays

`C_i^D=(gamma/2)d_c(x_i,h_i)^2`.

The anchors remain

`h=(1/6,1/2,5/6)`.

### Stage B — affine-demand Bertrand competition

The quadratic representative-consumer demand architecture is retained:

`max_{q>=0} a 1'q - (1/2) q'K(rho,s,x)q - p'q`.

Set

`K_ii=b`,

and for `i!=j`,

`K_ij = c0 + lambda*phi(x_i-x_j)/Tau_ij(rho,s) - v*M_ij(rho,s)`,

where

`phi(z)=[1+cos(2*pi*z)]/2`.

Nonnegative demand must continue to be defined through the quadratic program/KKT correspondence, never by negative-demand truncation.

### Stage W — welfare

Retain the explicit equal-country interpretation:

`W_i = CS/3 + p_i q_i - C_i^D`.

The equal one-third consumer-surplus allocation is an assumption of equal national consumer populations facing the common product market, not a consequence of the affine demand system itself.

### Stage S — stability

Before any Stage-4 stability theorem, freeze one explicit strict-blocking / consent correspondence for every admissible transition among `IS`, each `SU`, and `SW`. Each alternative partition must be evaluated at its own policy, repositioning, and pricing continuation equilibrium.

## 4. Why this is one mechanism, not two extensions

C1 does not append a separate policy benefit to the old depth variable. It replaces the source's depth-invariant off-diagonal compatibility intensity with realized technical interoperability generated by the same standards depth already chosen by the bloc.

The two economic consequences of depth are technologically linked:

1. higher depth makes products more technically interoperable within the bloc;
2. higher depth makes their standard-related characteristics less differentiated and can intensify competition.

This is the standard trade-off to be tested. The location stage then determines whether firms undo part of consequence 2 by differentiating along a separate characteristic dimension.

## 5. Reduced-form sign skeleton

Under symmetric IS and fixed anchor positions, let `phi_bar=1/4` and write

`c_FIX(s)=c0 + lambda*phi_bar/(t_bar-s) - v*chi(s)`.

Then

`dc_FIX/ds = lambda*phi_bar/(t_bar-s)^2 - v*chi'(s)`.

Stage 1 already established that, for the symmetric affine Bertrand continuation,

`dW_IS/dc < 0`.

Hence the source lower-bound result is no longer mechanical: the sign of the policy derivative depends on the relative marginal competition and interoperability effects.

In the FULL model, write pair proximity as `phi_ij(x(s))`. Then the direct policy effect is supplemented by the strategic repositioning response. For a within-bloc pair,

`d/ds [phi_ij(x(s))/(t_bar-s)]`

contains both the direct denominator effect and the induced change in pair proximity. If deeper standardization makes member firms move apart, the induced fall in `phi_ij` attenuates the competitive cost of depth. That induced term is absent in `B-FIX`.

This is the candidate source of a policy-threshold and coalition-stability difference. Stage 3 does not assume its sign is large enough.

## 6. Required nested benchmarks

### B-FIX

Same `rho`, `s`, `M(rho,s)`, `Tau(rho,s)`, pricing, welfare, and stability game, but fix `x=h`.

Purpose: isolate whether product repositioning changes optimal depth or blocking incentives.

### B-EXO

Allow endogenous positioning but fix all continuous depths at a pre-specified exogenous benchmark (including zero-depth and, separately, the historical fixed-positive witness when useful as diagnostics).

Purpose: isolate the Woeckener-type product-design response without endogenous policy adjustment.

### FULL

Endogenous partition comparison, bloc depth, product positioning, pricing, welfare, and stability.

Purpose: identify an interaction that neither B-FIX nor B-EXO can reproduce.

### Historical #65

Keep as a source benchmark with depth-invariant compatibility `G(rho)`. It is not nested by a result-targeted free parameter in the Stage-4 baseline; it is the documented predecessor whose policy-adjustment absorption motivated C1.

## 7. Candidate propositions for Stage-4 kill testing

These are targets, not current claims.

### P1 — Global continuation

Under transparent primitive inequalities, the nonnegative quadratic demand and pure-strategy Bertrand continuation exist and are unique for every admissible `(rho,s,x)` history in the Stage-4 domain.

Failure -> `NO-GO` or return within Stage 4 before any economics claim.

### P2 — Standards-contingent repositioning

There exists a nondegenerate parameter region in which a positive-depth coalition induces a strict product-position response while at least one relevant benchmark does not; the response must survive whole-circle unilateral best-response checks.

Failure -> C1 cannot make positioning indispensable.

### P3 — Policy absorption / non-absorption threshold

Characterize a condition comparing the marginal realized-interoperability gain with the marginal competitive effect of depth. Show how the condition differs between B-FIX and FULL because product repositioning changes effective pair proximity.

A mere positive `s*` is not sufficient.

### P4 — Repositioning-essential coalition result

At least one of the following must hold on an open, pre-specified regular parameter region:

- B-FIX and FULL have different stable standards partitions;
- a strict coalition-stability/blocking threshold differs between B-FIX and FULL;
- a coalition blocking relation reverses because of repositioning;
- repositioning creates a private/social stability wedge absent in B-FIX.

Removing product repositioning must destroy the headline result.

Failure -> **TERMINATE THE REVIVAL**. Do not add asymmetry, multi-market structure, nonlinear `chi`, bargaining, or another primitive inside the same Stage-4 attempt.

## 8. Stage-4 parameter discipline

The first Stage-4 pass must use transparent, pre-specified parameter restrictions chosen for regularity and economic interpretation, not to obtain `SU > IS` or any particular stable partition.

Allowed:

- scale normalizations;
- parameter inequalities needed for positive definiteness, positive quantities, and well-defined global continuation;
- a coarse pre-declared grid used to determine whether any open candidate region exists.

Prohibited:

- optimizing parameter values against the sign of `W_SU-W_IS`;
- choosing nonlinear `chi` after observing failure;
- adding a policy cost to generate an interior solution;
- adding home bias / heterogeneity / a second market after seeing the C1 result;
- treating a single numerical witness as the theorem.

## 9. Prior-art boundary

C1 does **not** claim novelty from continuous compatibility intensity, product differentiation after compatibility, or standards affecting firm strategy. Those are occupied by Klimenko, Woeckener, Huang–Tan–Teh–Zhou, Ruiz, and related work; own SSDI also occupies the generic continuous-standardization -> downstream-strategy -> policy-reversal architecture.

Only a FULL-only coalition-stability result or a clean absorption/non-absorption theorem tied indispensably to product repositioning can survive Stage 2.

## 10. Stage-3 selection verdict

**C1 REALIZED INTEROPERABILITY DEPTH -> GO TO STAGE 4 MINIMAL MODEL.**

C1 is selected because it corrects the economic meaning of the existing depth variable with one minimal interaction while preserving the central coalition/repositioning question. Selection does not certify that the desired theorem exists.
