## 1. Players and timing

There are three symmetric countries, each owning one firm, and three equal national consumer populations facing the same differentiated-product market.

The game is

`formal partition rho -> bloc depths s -> product positions x -> Bertrand prices p -> welfare -> coalition blocking/stability`.

Formal partitions are

`{IS, SU_12, SU_13, SU_23, SW}`.

Each standards bloc chooses one depth `s_C in [0,s_bar]` and maximizes the sum of national welfare of its members. Singleton blocs retain the later Stage-3 convention that they may choose specificity depth because this branch inherited that policy map from PR #65.

Firms then choose locations on the unit product-characteristic circle and pay quadratic repositioning cost around inherited anchors. Prices are nonnegative. Quantities solve the nonnegative quadratic consumer program.

## 3. Standards friction

The inherited pairwise friction map remains

`Tau_ij=t_bar-s_C` within one bloc `C`,

and

`Tau_ij=t_bar+(s_C+s_D)/2` across distinct blocs `C,D`.

For `SU_12`,

`Tau_12=t_bar-s_12`,

`Tau_13=Tau_23=t_bar+(s_12+s_3)/2`.

No policy cost is introduced.

## 4. C1 realized interoperability

The sole Stage-3-authorized change is

`chi(s)=s/s_bar`.

For `i!=j`,

- if `i,j` are in the same multi-country standards bloc `C`, set `M_ij=chi(s_C)`;
- if they are in different blocs, set `M_ij=0`.

There is no off-diagonal interoperability under `SW`. The realization map is linear and fixed before Stage 4. No nonlinear realization function, second policy instrument, home bias, policy cost, bargaining weight, market asymmetry, or extra market is allowed.

## 5. Product positioning

Firm `i` chooses `x_i` on the unit circle and pays

`C_i^D=(gamma/2)d_c(x_i,h_i)^2`.

Define

`phi(z)=[1+cos(2*pi*z)]/2`.

## 6. Consumer problem and Bertrand continuation

For fixed `(rho,s,x,p)`, quantities solve

`max_{q>=0} a 1' q - (1/2)q'Kq - p'q`,

where

`K_ii=b`,

`K_ij=c0 + lambda*phi(x_i-x_j)/Tau_ij(rho,s) - v*M_ij(rho,s)` for `i!=j`.

The KKT solution is the demand definition at all prices. Negative affine quantities are never accepted.

On the Stage-4 threshold domain `v in [0.06,0.16]`,

`c_min=c0-0.16=0.14`,

`c_max=c0+lambda/(t_bar-s_bar)=0.966666...`.

The source global inequalities remain satisfied:

`b>2c_max`,

`b*c_min>c_max^2`,

`b-2c_max+c_min>0`.

They imply the positive-definite / gross-substitute affine-demand architecture used for the globally nonnegative Bertrand continuation. The committed verifier additionally attacks representative histories using the KKT demand evaluator and direct global one-price deviations.

## 7. Profit and welfare

Firm payoff is

`Pi_i=p_i q_i-(gamma/2)d_c(x_i,h_i)^2`.

At the quadratic optimum,

`CS=(1/2)q'Kq`.

With three equal national consumer populations,

`W_i=CS/3+Pi_i`.

Foreign-firm profits are excluded from national welfare. This is an explicit ownership/population convention, not something inferred from the representative-consumer block.

## 8. Policy equilibrium

Each standards bloc maximizes the sum of its members' continuation welfare and re-solves the entire location and price continuation after a policy deviation.

In the economically relevant threshold region verified at Stage 4:

- IS selects `s_I=s_bar`;
- under each SU, both the member bloc and singleton outsider select `s_bar`;
- under SW, each singleton selects `s_bar`.

For IS, the upper-bound choice has an exact sufficient threshold. At symmetric anchors,

`c_IS(s)=c0+lambda*(1/4)/(t_bar-s)-v*s/s_bar`.

Because `dW_IS/dc<0`, IS welfare is increasing over the full policy interval whenever

`v > s_bar*lambda*(1/4)/(t_bar-s_bar)^2 = 1/18`.

Thus every blocking threshold reported below lies inside a region where the IS policy choice is analytically pinned at `s_bar`.

SU and SW upper-bound best responses are construction-verified by global policy scans with downstream location re-solution; Stage 4A must independently certify these policy statements.

## 10. Strict-blocking / residual-membership stability rule

Stage 4 freezes the previously ambiguous transition rule.

A deviating coalition may form its own exclusive bloc. Countries outside the deviating coalition preserve their existing coalition links whenever that residual link remains feasible. The deviation blocks iff every deviating country strictly gains after the alternative partition is re-solved at its own policy, location, and price equilibrium.

For three countries this generates:

- from IS: a pair may form its corresponding SU; a singleton may leave, leaving the residual pair as an SU;
- from `SU_ij`: either member may leave to produce SW; a cross-pair may form another SU; all three may form IS;
- from SW: any pair may form an SU and all three may form IS.

A coalition that would leave the partition unchanged is not a deviation.

This rule reproduces the historical intended comparisons: IS is pair-blocked by an SU only when both prospective members strictly gain; an SU is blocked by IS only when all three strictly gain.

## 13. Mechanism decomposition

C1 creates three distinct effects of deeper standards integration:

1. **realized interoperability benefit:** higher depth increases within-bloc `M_ij`;
2. **direct competitive compression:** lower within-bloc `Tau_ij` raises substitutability holding locations fixed;
3. **strategic re-differentiation:** member firms move apart, lowering `phi_ij` and attenuating the competitive compression.

Under SU, the outsider's endogenous specificity also raises cross-bloc `Tau`, which changes the return to the members' repositioning. The threshold ordering shows that neither policy adjustment alone (`B-FIX`) nor repositioning under the inherited exogenous-depth history (`B-EXO-HIST`) reproduces the FULL blocking region.
