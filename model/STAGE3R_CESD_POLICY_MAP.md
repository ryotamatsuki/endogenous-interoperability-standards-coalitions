# C-ESD Policy Map — Stage 3 Conditional-Gate Resolution

Date: 2026-09-04
Status: **FROZEN FOR STAGE 4 TEST**

## 1. Purpose

This note resolves the single architecture question left open by the C-ESD Stage-3R `CONDITIONAL GO`: how a formal standards partition and endogenous government policy jointly generate pairwise standard-induced differentiation frictions under IS, SU and SW, and who chooses the policy instrument.

The map is deliberately regime-neutral. It does not assign unrelated `t_IS`, `t_SU` and `t_SW` parameters by hand.

## 2. Objects

Let the formal standards partition be

`rho in {IS, SU_12, SU_13, SU_23, SW}`.

For each standards bloc `C in rho`, define one scalar

`s_C in [0, s_bar]`,

with

`0 <= s_bar < t_bar`.

Interpret `s_C` as the **coherence / specificity depth of bloc C's standard**.

- within a multi-country bloc, greater `s_C` makes the common standard more internally interoperable and reduces standard-induced adaptation friction;
- against a different bloc, greater specificity makes the standards more distinct and raises cross-bloc adaptation friction;
- for a singleton bloc, only the external-distinctiveness margin is operative.

The realized Hotelling/Salop friction is pairwise. Denote it by `tau_ij`.

## 3. Frozen pairwise-friction map

For firms/countries `i` and `j`, let `C(i)` be the standards bloc containing `i`.

Freeze

`tau_ij(rho,s) = t_bar - s_C` if `C(i)=C(j)=C`,

and

`tau_ij(rho,s) = t_bar + [s_C(i)+s_C(j)]/2` if `C(i) != C(j)`.

The map is symmetric: `tau_ij=tau_ji`.

No regime-specific slope parameter is introduced in the Stage-4 baseline.

## 4. Why the cross-bloc coefficient is 1/2

The coefficient `1/2` is a normalization, not a fitted mechanism parameter.

Consider `SU_12` and hold outsider specificity at zero. A marginal increase `ds_12` in bloc depth lowers the single within-bloc friction by exactly `ds_12`.

Require the SU integration-depth margin, by itself, to **redistribute** standard differentiation from the member-member relationship to the two bloc boundaries rather than mechanically change mean pairwise standard differentiation.

If each cross-bloc pair rises by `lambda ds_12`, preservation of the sum of the three pairwise frictions requires

`-1 + 2 lambda = 0`,

hence

`lambda = 1/2`.

Equivalently, among symmetric affine cross-bloc aggregators `t_bar + lambda(s_C+s_D)`, the equal-contribution normalization delivers `lambda=1/2`.

Stage 4 may later test a general `lambda` as robustness, but `lambda` is not a free baseline parameter.

## 5. Explicit regime maps

### IS

There is one bloc `N={1,2,3}` with depth `s_I`:

`tau_12=tau_13=tau_23=t_bar-s_I`.

Thus deeper international standardization uniformly lowers standard-induced pairwise differentiation.

### SU_12

There are two blocs, `{1,2}` and `{3}`, with depths `s_12` and `s_3`:

`tau_12 = t_bar-s_12`,

`tau_13 = tau_23 = t_bar + (s_12+s_3)/2`.

Therefore

`d tau_12/d s_12 = -1`,

`d tau_13/d s_12 = d tau_23/d s_12 = 1/2`.

Holding `s_3=0`,

`tau_12+tau_13+tau_23 = 3 t_bar`.

So deeper SU integration shifts differentiation away from the member-member pair and toward the member-outsider boundaries without changing mean pairwise friction.

The other SU partitions are generated by relabelling only.

### SW

There are three singleton blocs with depths `s_1,s_2,s_3`:

`tau_12=t_bar+(s_1+s_2)/2`,

`tau_13=t_bar+(s_1+s_3)/2`,

`tau_23=t_bar+(s_2+s_3)/2`.

National standard specificity therefore raises external standard-induced differentiation.

## 6. Zero-depth benchmark

For every partition,

`s_C=0 for all C`

implies

`tau_12=tau_13=tau_23=t_bar`.

This is the canonical **exogenous-policy / zero-depth benchmark** for C-ESD.

It is not claimed to reproduce the frozen B0 model algebraically. B0 remains the institutional IS/SU/SW coalition benchmark.

## 7. Network-effect treatment

Freeze the Stage-3 compatibility network `G_rho` separately from the continuous friction-depth map:

- IS: one common network;
- SU_12: firms 1 and 2 share a network, firm 3 is the outsider network;
- SW: three separate networks.

The baseline network-effect coefficient `v` does **not** depend on `s_C` at Stage 4.

Reason: C-ESD should first isolate the interaction between formal network membership, government-controlled standard-induced differentiation, and firm product repositioning. Making `v` itself a function of depth would add a second continuous policy channel and obscure the core mechanism.

A depth-dependent network benefit is reserved for later robustness only if the baseline survives.

## 8. Government / bloc policy game

After the formal partition `rho` is fixed, each standards bloc `C in rho` chooses its depth `s_C`.

Bloc C's objective is

`Omega_C = sum_{i in C} W_i`,

where each `W_i` is national continuation welfare after firms choose locations and downstream prices.

Blocs choose depths simultaneously, taking other bloc depths as given.

Thus the policy-depth equilibrium is a **Nash equilibrium among standards blocs**:

`s*(rho) in NE { max_{s_C in [0,s_bar]} Omega_C(rho,s) }_{C in rho}`.

Consequences:

- IS: one bloc chooses `s_I` to maximize `W_1+W_2+W_3`;
- SU_12: bloc `{1,2}` chooses `s_12` to maximize `W_1+W_2`, while singleton `{3}` chooses `s_3` to maximize `W_3`;
- SW: each singleton `{i}` chooses `s_i` to maximize `W_i`.

In the symmetric main model, members of any multi-country bloc have identical continuation welfare. Therefore maximizing the bloc sum is equivalent to maximizing each member's common continuation welfare; no transfer or bargaining-weight assumption is required for the baseline.

## 9. Full Stage-4 timing

The frozen Stage-4 game is

`rho -> s*(rho) -> x*(rho,s*) -> p*(rho,s*,x*) -> W_i -> coalition stability`.

More explicitly:

1. governments form / are evaluated under a formal standards partition `rho`;
2. standards blocs play the depth-policy game and choose `s_C`;
3. firms observe the entire pairwise friction matrix `Tau(rho,s)` and choose product locations `x_i`;
4. firms compete in prices;
5. national continuation welfare is realized;
6. coalition blocking/deviation comparisons use the continuation equilibrium of each alternative partition.

## 10. National welfare

Retain the project objective

`W_i = CS_i + Pi_i`,

where `Pi_i` is the worldwide operating profit of country i's firm net of any substantive product-repositioning cost used in the anchored Salop model.

Foreign-firm profit is excluded from national welfare.

Stage 4 must define the national consumer-market allocation consistently with the Salop implementation before claiming any coalition-stability theorem.

No separate policy cost in `s_C` is introduced merely to manufacture an interior policy solution. Boundary policy equilibria are admissible.

## 11. Stage-4 nested benchmarks

Stage 4 must solve or characterize four objects using the same primitives:

- `B-EXO`: all `s_C=0`, endogenous product locations;
- `B-T`: endogenous bloc depths, product locations fixed at inherited anchors;
- `B-X`: exogenous zero-depth policy, endogenous locations;
- `FULL`: endogenous bloc depths and endogenous locations.

The contribution test is not whether `s*` and `x*` merely exist. The FULL architecture must generate a government-welfare / coalition-stability implication unavailable in `B-T` and `B-X` separately.

## 12. Institutional defense

The bloc-level policy rule is consistent with the standards-union interpretation: a formal standardization union has a common internal standard policy and a common stance toward nonmembers, while singleton countries retain national standard policy.

The baseline is intentionally symmetric and minimal. Asymmetric bargaining, transfers, pairwise recognition instruments, firm lobbying, relative-profit objectives and endogenous network intensity are excluded.

## 13. Freeze decision

**POLICY MAP PASSES THE STAGE-3 HARDENING GATE.**

The former ambiguity of a single scalar `t_SU` is removed. Under SU there is one scalar policy per standards bloc and one derived pairwise friction matrix. IS, SU and SW are all generated by the same rule.

C-ESD is therefore sufficiently specified to proceed to Stage 4 Minimal Model.
