# Idea Seed — Endogenous Interoperability and Standards Coalitions

Date: 2026-09-04
Status: pre-Stage-0 hypothesis record

## Phenomenon

Compatibility is often modeled as binary, but real standards can differ in how much room they leave for firm-specific differentiation and how strongly they reduce frictions between products or ecosystems.

A common standard may therefore create a degree of interoperability rather than an all-or-nothing compatibility state.

## Proposed explanation — hypothesis

Firms may choose interoperability strategically. Greater interoperability can expand effective network benefits and reduce consumer switching/mismatch frictions, but it may also intensify price competition, weaken differentiation, or reduce ecosystem-specific rents. These opposing forces may generate an interior private interoperability choice.

Governments or standards coalitions may value interoperability differently because they internalize consumer surplus, domestic producer surplus, network benefits, and possibly cross-border strategic effects. This can create a wedge between the privately chosen interoperability level and the level required for coalition stability or national welfare.

## Provisional research question

When firms can choose the degree of interoperability rather than only whether to adopt a common standard, how do network effects and product differentiation shape private interoperability choices, price competition, national welfare, and standards-coalition stability?

## Candidate strategic sequence

1. Formal standards/cooperation environment is determined or coalition membership is chosen.
2. Firms choose interoperability intensity `a_i` or another continuous compatibility instrument.
3. Effective pairwise interoperability is determined.
4. Firms compete in prices.
5. Consumers choose products under spatial differentiation, switching/mismatch frictions, and network benefits.
6. National welfare is evaluated.
7. Coalition stability is characterized by comparing membership/deviation incentives.

This sequence is provisional and may be reordered by Stage 3/4.

## Candidate mechanisms

### M1 — Competition-intensification mechanism

Higher interoperability reduces effective differentiation or switching frictions, increasing demand substitutability and intensifying price competition. Firms may therefore underprovide interoperability relative to consumers or governments.

### M2 — Network-expansion mechanism

Higher interoperability enlarges the effective network accessible to users, raising demand value. This creates a positive private and social return to interoperability.

### M3 — Differentiation / ecosystem-rent mechanism

Lower interoperability can preserve ecosystem-specific rents or soften competition. This may counteract network-expansion incentives and support an interior choice if the trade-off is economically well founded.

### M4 — Coalition-threshold mechanism

A standards coalition may be stable only when interoperability exceeds or falls below a threshold `â`. If the private choice `a_o*` crosses this threshold as parameters change, endogenous interoperability can alter coalition formation rather than merely equilibrium prices.

### M5 — International-welfare wedge

Governments may value interoperability differently from firms because national welfare internalizes domestic consumer surplus and producer surplus but not necessarily foreign surplus. This could create cross-country incentives for formal standardization distinct from firms' technical compatibility choices.

## Immediate kill risks

- Endogenous `a_i` is corner-valued except under artificial curvature.
- Network effects simply shift demand without changing the strategic compatibility margin.
- Salop differentiation plus continuous interoperability is a reparameterization of transport cost.
- The government threshold `â` is algebraically identical to the private FOC.
- Coalition stability does not change once interoperability is endogenous.
- Existing endogenous-compatibility/network-effects models already contain the whole game after relabeling.

## Required early success

The strongest early signal is a minimal, economically interpretable structure that yields a nonempty parameter set with:

- `0 < a_o* < 1`, and
- a distinct coalition/welfare threshold `â`, with
- changes in the ordering `a_o* ≷ â` producing a genuine strategic or welfare regime change.

Until that survives, no full-paper architecture is justified.
