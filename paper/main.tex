\begin{abstract}
We study international standards coalitions when governments inside a non-singleton coalition choose additional within-coalition harmonization depth and firms subsequently choose horizontal product positions. Deeper regional integration compresses standard-based differentiation among member firms while increasing relative separation from outsiders. Member firms can respond by strategically re-differentiating on product characteristics left outside the standard. In a three-country Salop model with network effects, we identify a constructive regular region in which endogenous harmonization depth alone and endogenous product positioning alone each leave international standardization stable, whereas their joint endogeneity makes regional standardization unions stable. The reversal is driven by domestic producer-rent gains that outweigh member consumer losses. At the canonical witness, the stable regional union is globally welfare-inferior to international standardization, and firms re-differentiate more than a constrained social planner. Whole-circle continuation validity over the repaired feasible policy domain is computationally verified at the canonical primitives. The contribution is an interaction result rather than a claim that compatibility policy, endogenous differentiation, or partial standards unions are individually new.
\end{abstract}
