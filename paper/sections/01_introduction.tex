\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

International standardization reduces technical incompatibility, expands network reach, and can lower adaptation costs. But standardization also changes the competitive environment faced by firms. When a regulated interface becomes more homogeneous, firms need not remain equally homogeneous along every other product dimension. They can reposition products, redesign features, or otherwise re-create horizontal differentiation outside the standardized interface. This paper asks whether that post-policy response can change the political stability of international standards coalitions.

The question matters because standards policy is often evaluated holding products fixed. That can be misleading when firms retain discretion over attributes not covered by the standard. A regional standards union can compress standard-based differentiation among member firms and increase relative separation from outsiders, while simultaneously changing members' incentives to differentiate on remaining product dimensions. The relevant government comparison is therefore not only between standards architectures, but also between the continuation equilibria those architectures induce.

We study a three-country model in which governments first form a formal standards regime---international standardization, a two-country standardization union, or a standards war. Conditional on that regime, each non-singleton standards coalition chooses an additional within-coalition harmonization depth. A singleton bloc has no internal harmonization-depth instrument. Deeper coalition integration lowers pairwise standard friction inside the bloc and raises relative friction across its boundary. Firms observe the resulting friction structure, choose horizontal product locations on a Salop circle at a real redesign cost, and then compete in prices in the presence of network effects. National governments value consumer surplus and domestic producer profits, and coalition stability is evaluated using the regime-specific continuation equilibrium.

The main result is a post-policy strategic re-differentiation coalition-stability reversal. The central comparison holds the standards architecture fixed and changes whether firms may reposition afterward. In benchmark B-T, coalition harmonization depth is chosen as in the model but product locations remain at inherited anchors. At the canonical witness, a prospective member prefers international standardization to a regional standards union in this fixed-position evaluation. In the full game, firms can reposition after policy; union members move apart on the remaining horizontal dimension, domestic producer rents increase, and the member-country ranking reverses. The regional union becomes stable and international standardization becomes pair-blockable.

A hostile identification benchmark clarifies what the result does and does not show. Benchmark B-EQ fixes the harmonization depths of non-singleton coalitions exogenously at the values selected in the full game while keeping product locations endogenous. At the canonical witness, both international and regional harmonization choices are at the cap, so B-EQ is the same downstream game as FULL and yields the same positive member gain from the regional union. Endogenous harmonization choice is therefore not necessary for the reversal. The relevant causal margin identified by the benchmark comparison is the post-policy product-position response. A second benchmark, B-X0, fixes additional harmonization depth at zero while keeping locations endogenous; it remains useful descriptively but is not an identification test for the necessity of policy endogeneity.

The result is constructive rather than a global classification. The location game on a circle can exhibit order-changing deviations, so policy-stage values must be built from genuine downstream location equilibria rather than fixed-order stationary points alone. Under the repaired v2 action set, the canonical primitives are computationally audited over the full feasible $IS$ and $SU$ policy domains: continuation candidates are checked against whole-circle unilateral location deviations before the scalar policy optimum is selected. The headline welfare inequalities are strict, and conditional on persistence of the selected regular continuation branch, continuity yields a nonempty local parameter region. We do not claim a global parameter-space theorem or generality beyond the verified model and parameter evidence.

The welfare mechanism is distributional. For a prospective union member,
\[
\Delta_M=\Delta\Pi_M+\frac{\Delta CS}{3}.
\]
At the canonical witness, the member country's consumer-surplus component is negative, whereas the domestic-profit component is positive and slightly larger. The political attraction of the regional union therefore comes from domestic producer-rent capture rather than from a consumer gain. At the same witness, international standardization yields higher global welfare than the stable regional union because price transfers cancel globally and the remaining real terms favor the broader standard. The decentralized coalition game can thus regionalize standards even when world welfare favors international integration.

A second welfare comparison concerns product positioning itself. After the regional standard is fixed, a constrained social planner also chooses some re-differentiation, so the private response is not purely wasteful. Private firms nevertheless move farther apart than the constrained social benchmark. Standard integration therefore creates a margin on which strategic competition can partially undo the competitive consequences of the policy and can do so excessively from a social perspective.

The contribution is not the introduction of standards coalitions, continuous harmonization, or endogenous product differentiation separately. \citet{GandalShy2001} already analyze three-country standardization unions, network effects, national welfare, and coalition incentives. \citet{Ruiz2004} already studies government standards policy followed by endogenous product characteristics and price competition. \citet{TakaradaEtAl2020} analyze regional versus multilateral standards harmonization, national welfare, and blocking with an endogenous common regional standard, while \citet{KawabataTakarada2021} study deep trade agreements and standards harmonization. Continuous compatibility policy and modern interoperability research provide further close precedents. Our result concerns the equilibrium-ranking consequence of the continuation response: relative to an evaluation that holds product positions fixed, strategic post-policy re-differentiation can reverse member governments' ranking of international standardization and a regional standards union and thereby change coalition stability.
