\section{Model}\label{sec:model}

We consider three symmetric countries, indexed by $i\in\{1,2,3\}$, each of which owns one domestic firm. Consumers have unit mass and are uniformly distributed on a unit Salop circle. Country of residence is independent of product taste, so each country contains one third of the consumer population. Production has zero marginal cost. The model separates two dimensions that are often bundled together in compatibility models: the degree of standard integration chosen by governments and the horizontal product characteristic chosen by firms.

\subsection{Standards regimes and policy depth}

A formal standards regime is a partition
\[
\rho\in\{IS,SU_{12},SU_{13},SU_{23},SW\},
\]
where $IS$ is international standardization, $SU_{ij}$ is a two-country standardization union with the remaining country outside, and $SW$ is a standards war with three singleton blocs. The policy variable $s_C$ measures \emph{additional within-coalition harmonization depth} conditional on the formal regime. A non-singleton bloc $C$ chooses
\[
s_C\in[0,\bar s],
\qquad
0\leq \bar s<\bar t,
\qquad \bar t>0,
\]
whereas a singleton bloc has $s_C=0$ by definition and has no positive harmonization-depth instrument.

The baseline friction between products $i$ and $j$ is transformed by bloc depth according to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau-map}
\tau_{ij}(\rho,s)=
\begin{cases}
\bar t-s_C, & i,j\in C,\\[2mm]
\bar t+\dfrac{s_C+s_D}{2}, & i\in C,\ j\in D,\ C\neq D.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thus deeper integration compresses standard-based differentiation inside a non-singleton bloc while raising relative separation across its bloc boundary. The coefficient $1/2$ is a symmetric mean-preserving normalization; the economic restriction is the within-versus-cross-bloc asymmetry, not that particular coefficient.

For example, under $SU_{12}$ the member bloc chooses $s_{12}\in[0,\bar s]$ while outsider country 3 is a singleton and therefore has $s_3=0$. Hence
\[
\tau_{12}=\bar t-s_{12},\qquad
\tau_{13}=\tau_{23}=\bar t+\frac{s_{12}}{2}.
\]
An increase in $s_{12}$ simultaneously lowers the standard friction between member firms 1 and 2 and raises their standard friction relative to outsider 3. Under $IS$, the grand coalition chooses a single depth $s_I\in[0,\bar s]$. Under $SW$, every bloc is a singleton, so there is no continuous depth choice and all $s_C$ equal zero by definition.

Whenever a non-singleton bloc $C$ is present, its governments jointly choose depth to maximize the sum of national welfare of its members,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bloc-objective}
\Omega_C=\sum_{i\in C}W_i.
\end{equation}
There is no direct policy cost.

\subsection{Product positioning and consumers}

Firm $i$ inherits an anchor
\[
h=\left(\frac16,\frac12,\frac56\right)
\]
and chooses a product location $x_i$ on the circle. Repositioning entails the real redesign cost
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reposition-cost}
C_i^D=\frac{\gamma}{2}d_c(x_i,h_i)^2,
\qquad \gamma>0,
\end{equation}
where $d_c$ denotes circular distance.

Consider an arc of length $\ell_{ij}$ between adjacent products $i$ and $j$. A consumer located a distance $y$ from product $i$ obtains
\begin{align}
 u_i(y)&=A-p_i-\tau_{ij}y+vn_i,\\
 u_j(y)&=A-p_j-\tau_{ij}(\ell_{ij}-y)+vn_j,
\end{align}
where $A$ is sufficiently large for full market coverage, $p_i$ is price, $v>0$ is the network-effect parameter, and $n_i$ is the network size relevant to product $i$.

The formal regime determines the compatibility graph. Let $q=(q_1,q_2,q_3)'$ denote market shares and write
\[
n=G_\rho q.
\]
The network matrices are
\[
G_{IS}=\mathbf 1\mathbf 1',\qquad
G_{SW}=I,
\]
and, for $SU_{12}$,
\[
G_{SU_{12}}=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&0\\
1&1&0\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix},
\]
with the other unions defined by relabeling. The network coefficient $v$ is fixed conditional on this graph; policy depth changes standard friction but not $v$ itself.

The consumer indifferent between adjacent products $i$ and $j$ is located at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:indifferent}
y_{ij}=\frac{\ell_{ij}}{2}
+\frac{p_j-p_i+v(n_i-n_j)}{2\tau_{ij}}.
\end{equation}
We restrict attention to regular equilibria in which every such indifferent consumer lies strictly inside the relevant arc.

\subsection{Firms, governments, and timing}

Firm $i$ earns net profit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profit}
\Pi_i=p_iq_i-C_i^D.
\end{equation}
Aggregate consumer surplus is denoted by $CS$. Because each country contains one third of the consumer population and owns one domestic firm, national welfare is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:national-welfare}
W_i=\frac{CS}{3}+\Pi_i.
\end{equation}
Foreign producer profits are excluded from $W_i$.

The sequence of play is
\[
\rho
\;\longrightarrow\;
\{s_C\}_{C\in\rho:\,|C|\geq2}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\Tau(\rho,s)
\;\longrightarrow\;
\{x_i\}_{i=1}^3
\;\longrightarrow\;
\{p_i\}_{i=1}^3.
\]
All previous-stage choices are observed. Conditional on the partition, each non-singleton standards bloc chooses its additional harmonization depth; singleton blocs have no depth action. In the three-country regimes considered here, this leaves one scalar policy choice under $IS$, one scalar policy choice under an $SU$, and no depth choice under $SW$. Firms then choose product locations and price competition follows. We solve the continuation game by backward induction and evaluate regime stability using the resulting national welfare levels.

The equilibrium concept is subgame-perfect Nash equilibrium together with strict-blocking, exclusive-membership stability across $IS$, the three $SU$s, and $SW$. Policy-stage comparisons use downstream continuation equilibria that satisfy the whole-circle location best-response requirement described in Section \ref{sec:equilibrium}. In particular, $IS$ is blocked by an $SU$-forming pair if both prospective members strictly prefer the union; an existing $SU$ is blocked by $IS$ only if all three countries strictly gain from moving to $IS$.
