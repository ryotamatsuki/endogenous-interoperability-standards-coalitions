\section{Equilibrium Characterization}\label{sec:equilibrium}

We solve the price and product-location stages for a fixed formal regime $\rho$ and a fixed policy-induced friction matrix $\Tau$. The key advantage of the spatial representation is that heterogeneous bilateral frictions can be written compactly using a weighted graph Laplacian.

\subsection{Demand and price competition}

Let $L(\Tau)$ denote the weighted Laplacian with edge weights $1/\tau_{ij}$, and let $b(x)$ be the zero-price, zero-network Voronoi share vector generated by the current product locations. Summing the arc-level indifferent-consumer conditions in \eqref{eq:indifferent} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-demand}
q=b(x)-\frac12L(\Tau)p+\frac v2L(\Tau)G_\rho q.
\end{equation}
Define
\[
A_\rho=I-\frac v2L(\Tau)G_\rho,
\qquad
D=-\frac12A_\rho^{-1}L(\Tau).
\]
Whenever $A_\rho$ is nonsingular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:demand-reduced}
q=A_\rho^{-1}b+Dp.
\end{equation}

Firm $i$ chooses $p_i$ to maximize $p_iq_i$, taking product locations and rivals' prices as given. The first-order condition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:price-foc}
q_i+D_{ii}p_i=0.
\end{equation}
On the regular domain $D_{ii}<0$, let
\[
M=\operatorname{diag}\left(-\frac1{D_{11}},-\frac1{D_{22}},-\frac1{D_{33}}\right).
\]
Then $p=Mq$. Substitution into \eqref{eq:demand-reduced} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:price-system}
q=Kb,
\qquad
K=(I-DM)^{-1}A_\rho^{-1},
\qquad
p=Mq.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Regular price equilibrium]\label{prop:price}
Fix $(\rho,\Tau,x)$. Suppose $A_\rho$ and $I-DM$ are nonsingular, $D_{ii}<0$ for all $i$, and the implied prices, quantities, and arc shares are strictly positive and interior. Then the price subgame has a unique interior Nash equilibrium characterized by \eqref{eq:price-system}.
\end{proposition}

The proposition is an exact analytic result on the stated regular domain. In the symmetric $IS$ and $SW$ cases it reproduces the closed forms used in the computational verification. In particular, the symmetric $SW$ price is positive only if $2t-3v>0$.

\subsection{Product-location stage}

For a fixed cyclic ordering $1\to2\to3\to1$, the Voronoi shares are affine in locations:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:b-linear}
b(x)=b_0+Bx,
\end{equation}
where
\[
b_0=\begin{pmatrix}1/2\\0\\1/2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}
0&1/2&-1/2\\
-1/2&0&1/2\\
1/2&-1/2&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:price-system}, write
\[
q=c+Rx,
\qquad c=Kb_0,
\qquad R=KB.
\]
Because $p_i=-q_i/D_{ii}$, operating profit can be written as $M_{ii}q_i^2$. Hence, on a fixed cyclic-order branch,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:location-profit}
\Pi_i=M_{ii}(c_i+R_ix)^2-\frac\gamma2(x_i-h_i)^2.
\end{equation}
The conditional first-order and second-order conditions are
\begin{align}
2M_{ii}R_{ii}(c_i+R_ix)-\gamma(x_i-h_i)&=0,\label{eq:location-foc}\\
2M_{ii}R_{ii}^2-\gamma&<0.\label{eq:location-soc}
\end{align}
The FOC system is linear in $x$ and therefore gives a closed-form conditional location candidate whenever the associated matrix is nonsingular.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional location system]\label{prop:location}
For any fixed cyclic ordering and regular downstream price system, an interior location candidate satisfying \eqref{eq:location-foc} is characterized by a linear system. If \eqref{eq:location-soc} holds for every firm, this candidate is a strict local best response within that ordering.
\end{proposition}

A local candidate is not sufficient for equilibrium on the circle. A firm can jump across a rival and change the cyclic ordering. Accordingly, throughout the main result we impose an additional global-best-response requirement: no firm may profitably relocate to any point on the circle after re-solving the price subgame. The canonical witness and all reported neighborhood checks satisfy this condition. Low-redesign-cost parameterizations that fail the whole-circle check are excluded rather than repaired.

\subsection{Policy stage and regime values}

Let $x^*(\rho,s)$ and $p^*(\rho,s)$ denote a regular continuation equilibrium that satisfies both the conditional location conditions and the whole-circle deviation test. A standards bloc $C$ chooses $s_C$ to maximize
\[
\Omega_C(s_C,s_{-C};\rho)=\sum_{i\in C}W_i\bigl(\rho,s,x^*(\rho,s),p^*(\rho,s)\bigr).
\]
The policy profile $s^*(\rho)$ is a Nash equilibrium among blocs. The resulting regime value for country $i$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regime-value}
V_i(\rho)=W_i\bigl(\rho,s^*(\rho),x^*(\rho,s^*),p^*(\rho,s^*)\bigr).
\end{equation}
These continuation values, rather than welfare evaluated at common exogenous policy values, determine coalition stability.
