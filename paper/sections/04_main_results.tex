\section{Main Results}\label{sec:results}

The contribution concerns the effect of post-policy product repositioning on governments' standards-coalition ranking. We therefore organize the comparison around one central fixed-position benchmark, one hostile identification benchmark, and one auxiliary benchmark.

\paragraph{Benchmark B-T.} Additional harmonization depth is endogenous for non-singleton blocs, but product locations are fixed at inherited anchors, $x_i=h_i$. This is the central fixed-product evaluation.

\paragraph{Benchmark B-EQ.} Harmonization depths of non-singleton blocs are exogenous and fixed at the values selected in FULL, while product locations remain endogenous. This benchmark tests whether endogenous harmonization choice itself is necessary for the reversal.

\paragraph{Benchmark B-X0.} Product locations are endogenous, but the additional harmonization depth of every non-singleton bloc is fixed at zero. Singleton blocs have zero depth by definition in all versions of the model. B-X0 is an auxiliary zero-additional-harmonization benchmark rather than the central identification comparison.

\paragraph{FULL.} Both non-singleton harmonization depth and product locations are endogenous.

For a symmetric prospective union, define the member gain relative to international standardization as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-member}
\Delta_M^k=W_M^{SU,k}-W^{IS,k}.
\end{equation}
The main question is whether allowing firms to reposition after a given standards architecture can change the sign of this national-welfare comparison relative to an evaluation that holds product positions fixed.

\subsection{Policy-induced re-differentiation}

Under $SU_{12}$, deeper union integration lowers $\tau_{12}$ while increasing $\tau_{13}$ and $\tau_{23}$. Holding product locations fixed, this compresses one source of differentiation between member firms 1 and 2. Because product location remains a separate strategic margin, the member firms can respond by moving apart on the circle. The canonical continuation equilibrium exhibits exactly this response.

At the canonical parameter vector
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witness}
(\bar t,v,\gamma,\bar s)=(1,0.04,0.11,0.25),
\end{equation}
the FULL policy choices are
\[
s_I^*=\bar s,
\qquad
s_{12}^*=\bar s,
\qquad
s_3=0\ \text{under }SU_{12}\text{ by the singleton action-set definition},
\]
and $SW$ has no continuous depth choice because all three blocs are singletons. Under $SU_{12}$, the product-location equilibrium is approximately
\[
x^{SU}=(0.084567,\,0.582100,\,0.833333).
\]
Relative to the inherited locations $(1/6,1/2,5/6)$, the two union members move outward while the outsider remains at its anchor. Thus the standardization union compresses standard-based differentiation inside the bloc but induces additional differentiation on the remaining horizontal product dimension.

\subsection{Post-policy repositioning and coalition reversal}

The central fixed-position benchmark and the full game rank the regimes differently. At \eqref{eq:witness},
\[
\Delta_M^{B-T}=-0.010167<0,
\qquad
\Delta_M^{FULL}=0.001571>0.
\]
Thus international standardization is preferred by a prospective member when product positions are held fixed, whereas the regional standards union becomes nationally preferred once firms can reposition after policy.

The hostile B-EQ benchmark sharpens the interpretation. At the canonical witness, FULL chooses the harmonization cap under both $IS$ and $SU$. Fixing those depths exogenously therefore leaves exactly the same downstream location and price game as FULL, so
\[
\Delta_M^{B-EQ}=\Delta_M^{FULL}>0.
\]
Endogenous harmonization choice is consequently not necessary for the reversal at the witness. The relevant identified margin is the post-policy product-position response.

For completeness, the auxiliary zero-additional-harmonization benchmark gives
\[
\Delta_M^{B-X0}=-0.000434<0.
\]
This comparison is informative about the zero-depth environment, but it does not establish that policy-depth endogeneity is necessary.

\begin{proposition}[Constructive post-policy coalition reversal]\label{prop:reversal}
There exists a nonempty regular parameter region in which
\[
\Delta_M^{B-T}<0,
\qquad
\Delta_M^{FULL}>0.
\]
In that region, a fixed-position evaluation favors international standardization, whereas allowing firms to reposition after standards policy makes the regional standards union nationally preferred and can render international standardization pair-blockable. At the canonical witness, $\Delta_M^{B-EQ}=\Delta_M^{FULL}>0$, so the reversal does not require endogenous harmonization-depth choice.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Status of Proposition \ref{prop:reversal}.}
The proposition is constructive rather than a global closed-form classification. At the parameter vector in \eqref{eq:witness}, the repaired v2 action set satisfies the regularity conditions used in the computation. In particular, for every feasible $IS$ and $SU$ harmonization depth at the canonical primitives, the verification searches the relevant cyclic-order and circular-anchor branches and checks whole-circle unilateral location deviations after re-solving the price subgame; the resulting policy choices are then obtained by global scalar optimization over the repaired action sets. The B-T and FULL inequalities are strict. Conditional on persistence of the selected regular continuation branch, continuity therefore implies a nonempty local neighborhood with the same sign pattern. We do not claim a global parameter-space theorem, nor do we claim that B-EQ reproduces FULL away from parameter values at which the fixed exogenous depths coincide with the full-game policy choices.

\subsection{Pre-specified local robustness}

The numerical reversal is small in FULL, so a single witness would leave open a knife-edge concern. Before re-running the model, we therefore pre-specified a nine-point local grid around \eqref{eq:witness}, varying the network-effect parameter $v$, redesign-cost curvature $\gamma$, and the harmonization cap $\bar s$ individually and jointly. At every point the verification re-solves the global B-T and FULL policy problems and checks the downstream continuation using the same whole-circle deviation standard as at the canonical witness. No failed or inconvenient point is dropped.

All nine points produce valid continuations and all nine preserve
\[
\Delta_M^{B-T}<0<\Delta_M^{FULL}.
\]
The exercise is deliberately local: it establishes that the sign reversal is not confined to the canonical calibration, but it is not a global comparative-statics or parameter-space classification. It also leaves the harmonization cap binding in the reported local grid, so the robustness evidence does not convert policy-depth endogeneity into a necessary mechanism.

The coalition-stability logic at the witness is transparent. In B-T, an $SU$ member is worse off than under $IS$, so no pair blocks international standardization. In FULL, $W_M^{SU}>W^{IS}$ while the outsider remains worse off than under $IS$ and members also prefer $SU$ to $SW$. Thus an $SU$ is not unanimously blocked by $IS$ and its members do not wish to dissolve it into $SW$. By symmetry, all three two-country unions are stable. B-EQ reproduces this downstream ranking at the witness despite removing policy-depth endogeneity.

The result therefore isolates a continuation-response mechanism rather than a two-endogeneity interaction. Standards harmonization changes effective competitive distances; firms then strategically re-differentiate on the remaining product dimension; the resulting competition-softening raises member producer rents enough to change the national-welfare and coalition-stability ranking relative to a fixed-position evaluation.
