\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper asks whether post-policy product repositioning can change governments' preferred standards coalition. In the model, a regional standards union compresses standard-based differentiation between its members, but member firms can respond by moving apart on another horizontal product dimension. That strategic re-differentiation softens competition and can raise domestic producer rents enough to reverse the member-country welfare ranking relative to an evaluation that holds product positions fixed.

The benchmark structure disciplines the interpretation. B-T favors international standardization at the canonical witness when product positions remain inherited. FULL permits post-policy repositioning and makes the regional union nationally preferred and stable. B-EQ fixes harmonization depths exogenously at their FULL values while retaining endogenous product locations and reproduces the same reversal at the witness. Endogenous harmonization choice is therefore not necessary for the identified mechanism. The pre-specified local robustness exercise preserves the sign reversal at all nine tested points around the canonical parameters.

The welfare source of the political reversal is distributional: domestic producer-rent gains slightly exceed member consumer losses, while international standardization yields higher world welfare at the canonical witness. Private firms also re-differentiate more than a constrained social planner after the regional standard is fixed.

The result is constructive rather than universal. It does not imply that regional standardization is generally inefficient or that all interoperability mandates induce product re-differentiation. It shows instead that fixed-product evaluations of standards policy can mis-rank coalition incentives when firms retain a strategic product-design margin after harmonization.
