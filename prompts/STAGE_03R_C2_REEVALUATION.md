## Objective

Re-evaluate C2 as a distinct mechanism after the Stage-4 NO-GO for C1. Do not treat bilateral implementation as a repair of the failed one-sided demand primitive.

Core question:

> Can a formal government standards coalition be destabilized because firms free-ride on one another's post-agreement bilateral interoperability implementation, so that regime-specific private implementation changes coalition participation/deviation incentives relative to fixed/full implementation?

## Binding prior results

- C1 / CSIC is terminated.
- Continuous compatibility, network effects, coalition formation, private/social wedges, pairwise interoperability, and compatibility policy are not standalone novelty claims.
- The desired contribution remains a full-game government-coalition stability result.
- A consumer-surplus model must have a coherent utility/inverse-demand microfoundation over unilateral implementation deviations.

## Mandatory C2 technology audit

Compare at least:

1. unilateral/substitutable converter provision (`max` or a smooth OR analogue);
2. mutual-veto / weakest-link implementation (`min` or product analogue);
3. independent pairwise public-good contributions;
4. a standard coalition-wide voluntary public good;
5. any variant that appears to create coalition-size underimplementation, and identify whether it actually imports capacity, scope costs, dilution, topology, or another Stage-3 mechanism.

Do not choose an aggregator because it generates the desired theorem.

## Closest prior-art threats

- Farrell & Saloner (1992), converters and interface control;
- Choi (1997), two-way converters and ignored positive externalities;
- de Palma, Leruth & Regibeau (1999), partial compatibility and the degree of consensus required for standardization;
- Garcia & Vergari (2016), explicit max-versus-min compatibility depending on veto power;
- Klimenko (2009), compatibility-enhancing effort plus government compatibility policy/international coordination;
- Malueg & Schwartz (2006), compatibility incentives with multiple rivals;
- modern pairwise/weighted interoperability literature already recorded as B5.

## Diagnostic full-game test

For the most defensible smooth bilateral technology, use the smallest B0-style symmetric Cournot diagnostic consistent with a representative utility. Compare IS with `SU_12` and test:

- whether implementation is actually lower in the larger coalition;
- whether a costless/full-interoperability benchmark and endogenous implementation have opposite government stability signs;
- whether any reversal is positive-measure and mechanism-driven rather than fixed-cost or implementation-cost accounting.

## Hard kill rule

Reject C2 if:

- natural bilateral technologies reproduce known converter/consensus games without a new coalition-size continuation effect;
- independent pairwise games have regime-invariant per-link implementation;
- standard public-good free-riding lowers individual effort but not effective aggregate interoperability as coalition size grows;
- the first natural smooth bilateral microfoundation yields no stability reversal;
- a positive result requires adding capacity constraints, scope diseconomies, averaging/dilution, coalition-specific costs, policy instruments, topology, dynamics, or another candidate mechanism.

## Output

Return one of:

- `GO -> GO TO MINIMAL MODEL` if one pure-C2 skeleton survives;
- `CONDITIONAL GO` only for one precisely diagnosed uncertainty;
- `NO-GO` if C2 itself has no defensible minimal route.
