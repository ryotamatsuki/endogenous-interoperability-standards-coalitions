## 1. Executive novelty verdict

**Canonical verdict: `GO` → `GO TO MECHANISM SEARCH`.**

**Route: GENERALIZATION / UNIFICATION CANDIDATE ONLY.**

The broad project is not novel. Stage 2 kills essentially every ingredient-level contribution claim:

- continuous/partial compatibility is old;
- network effects plus product differentiation plus compatibility choice is old;
- compatibility choice followed by price/quantity competition is old;
- private compatibility versus welfare is old;
- standardization unions among countries are old;
- endogenous standards coalitions among firms are old;
- coalition-proof partial compatibility is old;
- government continuous-compatibility policy and international coordination are old;
- modern weighted/coalitional interoperability networks and regulation are now an active 2025–2026 frontier.

The project survives only because no single prior model located in the Stage-2 search reproduces the full economically relevant game after direct relabeling:

`government coalition regime rho -> distinct private firms choose continuous implementation a*(rho) -> downstream competition -> national welfare V_i(rho) -> government participation/deviation stability`,

with a deviation `rho -> rho'` changing the continuation implementation equilibrium itself.

This is a narrow survival. It is **not** enough that the architecture combines known ingredients. Stage 3 is authorized solely to test whether the continuous implementation margin creates a full-game result that is unavailable in all nested benchmarks.

The minimum surviving candidate result is an **implementation-induced coalition-stability reversal**: a parameter region in which a formal coalition is stable under an exogenous/full or binary implementation benchmark but unstable under endogenous continuous private implementation, or vice versa, because `a*(rho)` and `a*(rho')` differ and change the sign of the national deviation payoff.

If Stage 3 cannot produce such a result with a minimal architecture, the project should be killed rather than enlarged.

## 3. Literature-family map

### Family A — private endogenous compatibility / interoperability

Representative sources:

- de Palma, Leruth & Regibeau (1999), *Partial compatibility with network externalities and double purchase*.
- Foros & Hansen (2001), *Competition and compatibility among Internet Service Providers*.
- Garcia (2016), *Revealing Incentives for Compatibility Provision in Vertically Differentiated Network Industries*.
- Stadler, Tobler Trexler & Unsorg (2022), *The Perpetual Trouble with Network Products Why IT Firms Choose Partial Compatibility*.
- Jeon, Menicucci & Nasr (2023), *Compatibility Choices, Switching Costs, and Data Portability*.
- Bourreau, Raizonville & Thébaudin (2026), *Interoperability Between Ad-Financed Platforms With Endogenous Multi-Homing*.

Conclusion: continuous compatibility and private under/over-provision are established objects. This family cannot support an ingredient-level novelty claim.

### Family B — compatibility alliances / coalitions among firms

Representative sources:

- Economides & Skrzypacz (2003), *Standards Coalitions Formation and Market Structure in Network Industries*.
- Toshimitsu (2018), *Strategic Compatibility Choice, Network Alliance, and Welfare*.
- Ding, Ko & Shen (2022), *Partial compatibility in two-sided markets: Equilibrium and welfare analysis*.

Conclusion: coalition formation, alliance stability, and coalition-proof partial compatibility are established. The word `coalition` is not a novelty source.

### Family C — government standardization unions / international standards

Representative sources:

- Gandal & Shy (2001), *Standardization policy and international trade*.
- Klimenko (2009), *Policies and international trade agreements on technical compatibility for industries with network externalities*.
- Klimenko (2009), *Strategic interoperability standards and trade taxes*.
- Geng (2019), *International Agreements on Product Standards under Consumption Externalities: National Treatment versus Mutual Recognition* — vertical standards, therefore adjacent rather than direct compatibility prior art.

Conclusion: governments, international agreements, national welfare, recognition, and partial compatibility policy are all known. Government participation is not new by itself.

### Family D — standards organizations and endogenous participation

Representative sources:

- Economides & Skrzypacz (2003).
- Fiedler, Larrain & Prüfer (2023), *Membership, governance, and lobbying in standard-setting organizations*.
- related SSO governance/standard-setting literature.

Conclusion: endogenous membership in standards bodies is known, but typically the same firms/innovators/implementers are coalition players and downstream market actors. This matters because the proposed project separates country coalition objectives from firm implementation objectives.

### Family E — current interoperability-network frontier

Representative sources:

- Ekmekci, White & Wu (2025), *Platform Competition and Interoperability: The Net Fee Model*.
- Kim (2026), *Data Portability and Interoperability Between Digital Platforms*.
- Bourreau, Raizonville & Thébaudin (2026).
- Huang, Tan, Teh & Zhou (2026), *A Network Approach to Interoperability*.

Conclusion: interoperability intensity/configuration, welfare, platform asymmetry, and coalitional network structures are a current frontier. Pairwise or weighted interoperability cannot be claimed as novel without a distinct upstream government-coalition interaction.

## 6. Whole-game absorption test

### 6.1 Is there one prior model that reproduces the full game?

**No single prior model located in the Stage-2 audit does so.**

The nearest attempts fail whole-game equivalence for different reasons:

- **B0 own frozen paper:** correct actors/objectives/timing/stability, but private compatibility/adoption is binary rather than a continuous intensive implementation margin.
- **Klimenko (2009):** continuous compatibility, government policy, international competition, national incentives, and an international agreement are present, but governments directly choose compatibility-related policy instruments; there is no endogenous standards-coalition membership whose deviation changes a separate private continuation implementation equilibrium.
- **Economides–Skrzypacz (2003):** standards coalitions are endogenous and oligopoly follows, but coalition members are the firms themselves, their objective is profit, and coalition affiliation itself determines the compatibility platform. There is no separate country government maximizing national welfare and no independent post-coalition continuous implementation control.
- **Ding–Ko–Shen (2022):** coalition-proof compatibility and welfare are present, but the same platforms are the strategic compatibility actors; government membership and national welfare are absent.
- **Gandal–Shy (2001):** government unions and national welfare are present, but compatibility/recognition is binary and the private continuous implementation continuation game is absent.

### 6.2 Is the candidate result an immediate corollary of an existing theorem?

Not yet. No audited theorem directly implies the sign of

`Delta_i(rho,rho';theta) = V_i(rho;theta) - V_i(rho';theta)`

when each continuation value uses a distinct endogenous continuous implementation equilibrium `a*(rho)` and `a*(rho')` controlled by firms.

However, this is a **high-risk corollary threat**: if a Stage-3 minimal model makes `a*(rho)` a monotone smooth transformation of B0's binary adoption payoff or of a Klimenko-style compatibility policy, the project is absorbed in substance even without an exact prior paper.

### 6.3 What strategic interaction appears only in the full architecture?

The only defensible candidate is the **regime-dependent private implementation feedback**:

`government regime rho -> private implementation best responses -> market equilibrium -> national continuation value -> government coalition deviation incentives`.

The government does not directly choose implementation. A deviation changes the set/institutional environment in which firms implement interoperability, so the government compares two endogenously different private continuation equilibria.

This is stronger than a simple private-versus-social wedge because the private wedge itself changes across the coalition and deviation regimes.

## 8. Backward / forward citation findings

### 8.1 Gandal–Shy → Klimenko

Klimenko explicitly contrasts his partial-compatibility policy model with Gandal–Shy's three-country recognition/standardization-union model. He identifies Gandal–Shy's compatibility as binary/full compliance while his own framework allows partial compatibility and active policies affecting compatibility. This confirms that the B2 versus B3 distinction is real and long recognized.

### 8.2 Classic compatibility lineage

The close papers trace back to Katz–Shapiro, Matutes–Regibeau, Farrell–Saloner, and partial-compatibility/interface/converter literature. This confirms that continuous or partial compatibility cannot be positioned as a new primitive.

### 8.3 Modern coalition-proof/platform lineage

Ding–Ko–Shen explicitly positions partial compatibility as a coalition-proof outcome in a three-platform setting and cites prior oligopoly compatibility work. This substantially weakens any claim based on coalition stability of private compatibility configurations.

### 8.4 Current 2025–2026 frontier

Current work now studies:

- interoperability regulation and platform competition (Ekmekci–White–Wu 2025);
- data portability plus interoperability (Kim 2026);
- endogenous platform interoperability with installed-base asymmetry (Bourreau–Raizonville–Thébaudin 2026);
- weighted/coalitional interoperability networks and welfare (Huang–Tan–Teh–Zhou 2026).

Therefore Stage 3 must not pivot toward pairwise/weighted platform interoperability merely to look modern. That frontier is already occupied.

### 8.5 SSO participation literature

Modern SSO work establishes endogenous membership and governance as separate research objects. It provides institutional support for separating participation from implementation, but it also means the project cannot claim that endogenous standards membership itself is new.

## 11. Surviving claim

Only the following claim is authorized for Stage 3 testing:

> **A government standards coalition can change firms' post-agreement continuous interoperability incentives; because a government deviation induces a different private implementation continuation equilibrium, endogenous implementation may reverse the sign of coalition participation/deviation incentives relative to binary or exogenous-implementation benchmarks.**

This is a candidate claim, not a result.

The preferred theorem target is:

### Implementation-induced stability reversal

Find conditions such that

`sign Delta_i^endo(rho,rho';theta) != sign Delta_i^benchmark(rho,rho';theta)`

for a nonempty parameter region, where `Delta_i^endo` substitutes regime-specific private continuous implementation equilibria and the benchmark fixes implementation or restricts it to a binary choice.

An especially strong version would show a non-monotone/disconnected stability region in a primitive parameter that cannot arise in B0.

## 12. Strongest prior-art threat

### Primary threat — B0 + Klimenko + Economides–Skrzypacz

The strongest referee attack is not one exact paper but an absorption argument:

1. B0 already supplies the full government coalition → private decision → competition → national welfare → stability skeleton.
2. Klimenko supplies continuous compatibility, firm compatibility effort, government policy and international coordination.
3. Economides–Skrzypacz supplies endogenous standards-coalition formation driven by the network-benefit versus competition trade-off.

A referee can therefore ask whether the project does anything more than insert Klimenko/Stadler-style continuous compatibility into B0, while reproducing the coalition intuition already present in Economides–Skrzypacz.

Stage 3 must answer this attack with a theorem, not with a longer literature discussion.

### Current-frontier threat — Huang et al. (2026)

If Stage 3 responds by adding pairwise topology or coalitional interoperability, Huang et al. becomes an immediate threat. The project should not use topology as a novelty escape hatch unless topology is strictly necessary for the surviving stability-reversal theorem.

## 13. Exact implication for mechanism search

Stage 3 is authorized under a narrow contract.

### Stage 3 may do

- search for the minimal way formal regime `rho` changes private continuous implementation incentives;
- derive regime-specific `a*(rho)` and `a*(rho')` in the smallest tractable game;
- test whether substitution into national continuation values changes the sign/ranking of coalition stability relative to B0/exogenous implementation;
- test one or two mechanism classes at most, with clear nested-benchmark recovery.

### Stage 3 may not do

- add Salop merely for differentiation;
- add network effects merely because compatibility papers use them;
- add pairwise topology merely to look distinct;
- add dynamics, installed bases, switching costs, multiple countries, or multiple interoperability instruments before the minimal reversal mechanism survives;
- manufacture interiority with arbitrary convex costs;
- revive any killed contribution claim.

### Preferred minimal mechanism-search question

> What is the smallest economically interpretable structure in which changing the formal standards regime changes firms' marginal benefit/cost of interoperability enough that `a*(rho)` differs from `a*(rho')`, and this continuation response changes the sign of at least one government's coalition deviation payoff relative to the binary/exogenous-implementation benchmark?

## 14. Final verdict and next-stage contract

**Verdict: `GO` → `GO TO MECHANISM SEARCH`.**

This verdict does not assert novelty. It asserts only that no single audited prior model reproduces the full game and that one economically coherent strategic feedback remains unabsorbed enough to justify a minimal Stage-3 mechanism search.

Stage 3 must preserve the nested-benchmark map and target an implementation-induced stability reversal or comparably sharp full-game result. If no such result emerges from a minimal model, return `NO-GO`; do not rescue the branch by adding complexity.
