## 3. Literature-family map

### A. Standards unions / coalition formation

Gandal & Shy (2001), Economides & Skrzypacz (2003/2004), Takarada (2020), and related trade-standardization papers already establish that coalition membership, compatibility, network effects, and competition can determine the equilibrium or stable standards regime.

Implication: **coalition stability in standards settings is not novel.**

### B. Compatibility followed by endogenous differentiation/design

Woeckener (1999) is binding prior art for the mechanism `compatibility first -> later product differentiation -> softened competition`. Baake & Boom (2001) and Ruiz (2004) further show that compatibility/standardization and endogenous product characteristics can interact strategically.

Implication: **post-standard product differentiation is not novel by itself.**

### C. Continuous government compatibility policy

Klimenko (2009) explicitly studies strategic government policies over partial technical compatibility in network industries.

Implication: **continuous standards/compatibility policy is not novel.**

### D. Costly product repositioning

Kishihara & Matsubayashi (2020), Gandhi et al. (2008), and later merger evidence/theory show that firms may reposition products away from inherited locations and that such repositioning can materially affect prices and welfare.

Implication: **repositioning costs / inherited anchors are infrastructure, not contribution.**

### E. Coalition -> endogenous downstream resource choice

Guo, Liu & Nault (2024) is a strong contemporary threat: coalition participation changes interoperability and the coalition subsequently selects resource investment, with equilibrium coalition size characterized.

Implication: **adding an endogenous post-coalition action is not enough.**

### F. Coalitional interoperability strength/configuration

Huang, Tan, Teh & Zhou (2026) study interoperability strength and configurations including coalition structures and derive pricing/welfare implications.

Implication: **coalitional interoperability structure and welfare effects are not enough.**

### G. Own internal prior art

`private-compatibility-standards-coalitions` already supplies formal government coalition structure, downstream private response, competition, welfare, and stability.

`standardization-scope-direction-innovation` already supplies continuous standardization policy, an endogenous downstream strategic reallocation, Bertrand competition, welfare, and a policy-ranking reversal.

Implication: the revival must not claim novelty from the generic architecture `continuous standardization policy -> endogenous firm response -> changed policy ranking`.

## 6. Whole-game absorption test

### Question 1 — one prior model reproduces the full game?

**NO IDENTIFIED EXACT MATCH.**

The full revival architecture requires simultaneously:

1. a multilateral standards partition / coalition state;
2. an endogenous bloc-level continuous standards-depth choice;
3. costly post-policy horizontal product repositioning;
4. downstream price competition;
5. national welfare accounting;
6. strict coalition blocking/stability;
7. a result comparing fixed-position and endogenous-position stability after policy optimization.

The searched literature distributes these pieces across different models with different players, objectives, and timing.

### Question 2 — is the desired result an immediate corollary?

**NO IDENTIFIED THEOREM MAKES IT IMMEDIATE.**

The Stage-1 absorption result itself is source-model-specific: IS membership supplies the full network benefit while depth increases substitutability without adding direct interoperability benefit. Existing papers show related policy adjustment, compatibility/differentiation, and coalition effects, but no located result proves a general absorption theorem for this standards-coalition/repositioning game.

### Question 3 — does the source model already contain a publishable residual result?

**NO.**

The current #65 source model yields absorption and leaves the stable coalition unchanged at the canonical witness. The Stage-2 GO is therefore not a claim that the source architecture is publishable. It authorizes Stage 3 to search for one minimal, independently motivated mechanism around the surviving question.

## 8. Backward / forward citation findings

The historical prior-art set remains valid but is incomplete without newer work.

Two additions materially tighten the frontier:

1. Guo, Liu & Nault (2024), because coalition interoperability and endogenous post-coalition resource choice jointly affect equilibrium coalition size.
2. Huang, Tan, Teh & Zhou (2026), because interoperability strength and coalition-based configurations jointly affect platform competition and welfare.

Neither paper contains the revival's product-repositioning + endogenous government depth + blocking/stability result, but both reduce the acceptable breadth of novelty claims.

The 2026 empirical standardization/innovation literature also reinforces that standards affect firm strategy, but it does not provide the theoretical coalition-repositioning result and therefore is motivational rather than absorbing prior art.

## 11. Surviving claim(s)

Exactly one research family survives:

> **Absorption vs non-absorption of strategic product repositioning in a standards-coalition game with endogenous bloc policy depth, where non-absorption has a coalition-stability consequence that disappears when product positions are fixed.**

Acceptable theorem forms include:

- a necessary/sufficient or sufficient-condition absorption theorem;
- a stability-threshold shift generated by repositioning;
- a blocking reversal between `B-FIX` and `FULL`;
- a stable-partition change on an open parameter region;
- a private/social coalition-stability wedge caused specifically by repositioning.

Stage 3 must not presuppose which form survives.
