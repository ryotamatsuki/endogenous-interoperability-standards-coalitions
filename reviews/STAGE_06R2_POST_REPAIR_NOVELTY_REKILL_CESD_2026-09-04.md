## 1. Executive novelty verdict

**CONDITIONAL GO — NARROWER CLAIM REQUIRED.**

**Novelty confidence: MEDIUM.**

No directly absorbing result was located in the audited search through 2026-09-04. In particular, no located paper establishes the following narrower result in an international standards-coalition game: holding the standards architecture/policy fixed, allowing firms to reposition on a separate horizontal product dimension after that policy can reverse member countries' IS-versus-SU national-welfare ranking and thereby stabilize a regional standards union.

However, the stronger Stage-6/Stage-8R wording — that *policy endogeneity* and *location endogeneity* are jointly necessary, with each endogenous margin alone selecting IS and only their interaction selecting SU — does **not** survive. Stage 11R's `B-EQ` benchmark fixes IS and SU harmonization depths exogenously at their FULL equilibrium values (`s_I=s_SU=s_bar`) while leaving locations endogenous. At the canonical witness both FULL policy choices are at the cap, so `B-EQ` is the same downstream game as FULL and therefore `Delta_M^(B-EQ)=Delta_M^(FULL)>0`. Policy endogeneity is thus not identified as a necessary margin at the witness.

Accordingly Stage 6R2 does **not** authorize the old headline `B-T<0, B-X<0, FULL>0` as evidence of a two-endogeneity interaction. `B-X` remains an informative zero-additional-harmonization benchmark only. The central identifying comparison is `B-T` versus FULL (or equivalently versus `B-EQ` at the witness): the same regional harmonization architecture evaluated with fixed product positions favors IS, whereas allowing post-policy strategic repositioning makes SU nationally attractive.

The surviving residual is therefore a **policy-induced strategic re-differentiation coalition-stability reversal**, not a claim that endogenous harmonization choice itself is essential.

## 3. Strongest single-paper threats

### 3.1 Ruiz (2004) — strongest post-policy strategic-response threat

Juan M. Ruiz, *Mix-and-Match and International Standardization Policy* (2004 working paper), is extremely close to the narrower C-ESD timing.

Ruiz has:

- governments choosing foreign-standard recognition policy;
- firms choosing product characteristics **after** government policy and **before** price competition;
- horizontal product differentiation;
- national welfare including domestic consumer surplus and domestic firm's profits at home and abroad;
- excessive strategic differentiation as firms soften price competition.

This permanently kills any broad claim that standards policy affecting endogenous product characteristics is new.

But Ruiz does **not** absorb the surviving result. It is a two-country recognition/trade game, not a three-country regional-versus-international standards-coalition stability game, and—most importantly—Ruiz explicitly reports that making product characteristics endogenous leaves the qualitative government-policy equilibrium intact. Endogenous positioning changes incidence and consumer comparisons, but it does not generate a regional-versus-international coalition-ranking reversal.

**Classification: STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE.**

Primary/full-text evidence located: author-uploaded full text, ResearchGate, `https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228458259_Mix-and-Match_and_International_Standardization_Policy`.

### 3.2 Gandal & Shy (2001) — strongest standards-union/network threat

Gandal and Shy, *Standardization Policy and International Trade*, Journal of International Economics 53(2), 363–383, DOI `10.1016/S0022-1996(00)00067-2`, already provide:

- three countries and three firms;
- government standards-recognition policy;
- standardization unions;
- mutual recognition among members and common policy toward the outsider;
- network effects and conversion costs;
- national welfare/participation incentives;
- parameter regions where a two-country union forms and world welfare falls relative to broader recognition.

This permanently kills novelty of SU itself, regional standardization stability, network effects, and the national-versus-world welfare conflict.

What it lacks is the firm stage that matters for the surviving C-ESD residual: product characteristics/locations do not endogenously reposition after coalition policy. Thus it contains no `policy -> horizontal repositioning -> national IS/SU ranking reversal` result.

**Classification: STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE.**

Publisher evidence: `https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-1996(00)00067-2`; accessible 1996 working-paper record: `https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/275615`.

### 3.3 Takarada, Kawabata, Yanase & Kurata (2020) — strongest repaired coalition-instrument/stability threat

This paper, *Standards Policy and International Trade: Multilateralism versus Regionalism*, Journal of Public Economic Theory 22(5), 1420–1441, DOI `10.1111/jpet.12425`, is a materially important addition to the old Stage-6 matrix.

It has a symmetric three-country model, endogenous continuous domestic standards, regional harmonization in which two member governments jointly choose a common regional standard to maximize joint welfare, multilateral harmonization in which all countries choose a common standard to maximize world welfare, country welfare containing domestic firms' profits and consumer surplus (net of externality loss), and the **core** as the blocking/stability concept. It shows that a world-welfare-maximizing multilateral standards agreement can fail to be in the core while only a regional agreement is in the core.

This means the repaired Stage-4R object — coalition-level common standards choice plus outsider plus regional/multilateral stability — is emphatically not setup novelty.

Yet firms respond only through downstream quantity choices; there is no post-policy endogenous horizontal product-characteristic/location stage, no network-interoperability graph, and no C-ESD repositioning-based reversal relative to a fixed-position benchmark.

**Classification: STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE.**

Publisher evidence: `https://doi.org/10.1111/jpet.12425`.

### 3.4 Kawabata & Takarada (2021) — deep regional agreement / harmonization-depth threat

*Deep Trade Agreements and Harmonization of Standards*, Southern Economic Journal 88(1), 118–143, DOI `10.1002/soej.12500`, analyzes a three-country oligopoly in which regional trade-agreement members jointly choose a common standard and a multilateral agreement chooses an international standard; standards and tariffs are endogenous. A deep FTA with harmonized standards can be a stumbling block to multilateral harmonization/free trade.

This further confirms that `insiders jointly choose a common intensive standards instrument while an outsider is outside that joint decision` is existing international-trade architecture. The mechanism, however, is standards/tariff/externality interaction rather than post-policy product repositioning.

**Classification: STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE on the repaired policy object; COMPONENT OVERLAP on the surviving headline.**

### 3.5 Klimenko (2009) — continuous compatibility-policy threat

Klimenko, JIE 77(2), 151–166, DOI `10.1016/j.jinteco.2008.08.005`, has continuous government compatibility policies, network externalities, international competition, national strategic policy incentives and international compatibility agreements. It kills continuous compatibility policy as a contribution. It does not add a post-policy product-location/design stage or an endogenous regional standards-union stability comparison.

**Classification: STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE.**

## 4. Compatibility × endogenous-differentiation literature

The re-audit confirms that the downstream ingredients are old:

- **Economides & Flyer (1997/98):** common compatibility standards trade network benefits against horizontal differentiation; incompatibility can soften competition. `COMPONENT OVERLAP`.
- **Economides & Skrzypacz (2003/04):** firms form standards coalitions before oligopoly; coalition size trades network gains against competition. `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE` to coalition/competition logic, but no governments or post-policy repositioning.
- **Matutes & Padilla (1994):** strict-subset ATM sharing or incompatibility can arise among three competing banks. `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE` to partial-compatibility coalition structure.
- **Baake & Boom (2001):** inherent quality is chosen, then compatibility/adapters, then prices; network effects and differentiation interact. Timing is the reverse of the C-ESD post-policy repositioning channel. `COMPONENT OVERLAP`.
- **Jonard & Schenk (1999/2003):** firms choose compatibility then prices; compatibility is a tradeoff between compatibility value and loss of differentiation. `COMPONENT OVERLAP`.
- **Wang & Lyu (2020):** endogenous horizontal product positioning under network effects, with differentiation affecting compatibility. `COMPONENT OVERLAP`.
- **Ding, Ko & Shen (2022):** three-platform partial compatibility can be the unique coalition-proof outcome; welfare can favor partial compatibility. `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE` to partial-compatibility stability, but no governments or product repositioning stage.
- **Stadler, Trexler & Unsorg (2022):** firms can jointly choose a common degree of compatibility/standardization before price competition and partial compatibility can be a SPNE. This is the closest located continuous common-standardization-intensity analogue to the repaired coalition-level instrument, but it is a two-firm firm-side SDO game, not a three-country welfare/stability game and has no endogenous product repositioning. `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE on instrument / COMPONENT OVERLAP on headline`.

None directly supplies the surviving national-coalition feedback.

## 5. 2024–2026 frontier attack

### Huang, Tan, Teh & Zhou (2026), *A Network Approach to Interoperability*

Current working paper/SSRN version (45 pages; February 2026) models interoperability as a weighted network, derives equilibrium prices and welfare, and compares industry-wide with coalitional interoperability. Coalitional interoperability may be consumer-optimal when user externalities are strong even though industry-wide full interoperability maximizes total welfare.

This is the strongest current frontier paper on **interoperability network configuration + prices + welfare**, and therefore kills any broad C-ESD claim about coalitional interoperability having distinct welfare effects.

It still does not absorb C-ESD. Its current paper explicitly states that it focuses on price competition and leaves **endogenous formation of interoperability coalitions** for future research. It also has no post-policy endogenous horizontal product-location stage and no country/government IS/SU stability.

**Classification: STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE / CURRENT FRONTIER.**

Sources: SSRN `https://ssrn.com/abstract=6244719`; current author page `https://tehtathow.com/research/`.

### Kretschmer, Rasch, Shekhar & Wenzel (2025), *Strategic Response to Mandated Interoperability: Privacy Spillovers in Network Markets*

This paper directly kills any generic claim that mandated interoperability has not been studied with strategic downstream firm adaptation. The incumbent responds to interoperability by increasing data collection; privacy spillovers may lower entrant share and user welfare.

The strategic response is not horizontal product repositioning and the model has no regional standards coalition, national welfare coalition incentives, or coalition-stability reversal.

**Classification: COMPONENT OVERLAP / CURRENT FRONTIER.**

Source: SSRN `https://ssrn.com/abstract=5516420`.

### Other current frontier

- **Li, Sun, Song & Wu (2026), *From Centrality Discounts to Centrality Premia: Interoperability and Platform Competition in Social Networks*, arXiv:2607.09269:** interoperability can reverse pricing effects across network positions, but there is no government coalition/stability/product-location feedback. `BACKGROUND / COMPONENT OVERLAP`.
- 2026 DMA institutional/competition work confirms that mandated interoperability and adversarial standard setting are active current topics, but no located economics result reproduces the C-ESD national coalition-ranking feedback.

## 6. Ruiz + Gandal–Shy synthesis reconstruction attack

### Hostile reconstruction

A skeptical referee can plausibly say:

> Take Gandal–Shy's three-country standards-union game, with governments evaluating national welfare and deciding whether to recognize outsider standards. Insert Ruiz's stage after policy: firms choose horizontal product characteristics to soften subsequent price competition. The resulting model appears to be C-ESD in different notation.

This objection is very strong at the **architecture** level.

### 6.1 What comes directly from Gandal–Shy

Use Gandal–Shy's three-country/three-firm environment, standards-union membership, mutual recognition within a union, common policy toward outsiders, network/conversion-cost effects, and national-welfare participation incentives. Those objects already deliver IS/SU comparisons and regionalization under some parameters.

### 6.2 What comes directly from Ruiz

Append a firm product-characteristic stage between government policy and prices. Ruiz gives the strategic motive: under competition, firms can choose characteristics to soften price rivalry; governments evaluate the resulting domestic CS plus domestic worldwide firm profit.

### 6.3 Does the C-ESD reversal follow automatically?

**No.** The existing propositions do not imply its sign.

First, Ruiz's own exercise is the direct warning against automatic inference: after adding endogenous product characteristics, his qualitative government-policy equilibrium remains robust rather than reversing. Thus `government policy -> endogenous differentiation` does not by itself imply a policy-ranking reversal.

Second, Gandal–Shy has no mapping from a standards-union policy to a separate repositioning game and therefore no theorem saying that the induced producer-rent response will cross the member-country welfare threshold against IS.

To obtain the C-ESD result, one must solve a new continuation in which a regional standards policy compresses the standards dimension while firms can re-differentiate on another horizontal dimension, calculate the induced price/profit/CS effects, and establish that the member producer-rent gain exceeds the consumer loss sufficiently to change the strict-blocking comparison. That sign is not a corollary of either paper.

### 6.4 What the synthesis attack nevertheless kills

It makes almost all setup language non-contributory. C-ESD cannot claim novelty from combining governments, standards unions, compatibility/network effects, post-policy product differentiation, national welfare, or price competition. Only the **result-level ranking feedback** is defensible.

### 6.5 Composite classification

**Ruiz + Gandal–Shy: STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE COMPOSITE, but not NEAR-ABSORBS under the workflow kill rule.** The synthesis requires a nontrivial new equilibrium and welfare-sign result; it is not obtained by simply quoting and stacking existing propositions.

## 7. Post-Stage-4R repair attack

The repaired action set is not independently novel.

Two newly emphasized prior-art families are especially close:

1. **Takarada et al. (2020):** two regional member governments jointly choose a common regional standard; all three can choose a multilateral standard; outsider remains outside the regional joint decision; blocking/core selects regional versus multilateral standards regimes.
2. **Kawabata & Takarada (2021):** RTA/CU members jointly determine common standards while an outsider remains outside the regional common-standard decision; multilateral harmonization is compared with regional deep agreements.
3. **Stadler et al. (2022):** firms jointly choose a common degree of compatibility/standardization before prices, demonstrating that a continuous common-standardization intensity is also old in IO/network markets.

Therefore the Stage-4R repair moves C-ESD closer to existing coalition/common-standard models. It does **not**, however, spatially relabel one of them: the closest trade papers do not have the post-policy horizontal repositioning stage, and Stadler et al. lacks governments/national welfare/regional-vs-international blocking.

**Kill 4 does not trigger, but setup novelty is zero.**

## 8. Result-built-into-assumption attack

The C-ESD SU map has

`tau_12 = t_bar - s`,

`tau_13 = tau_23 = t_bar + s/2`.

The exact `1/2` coefficient remains a stylized normalization and is not a contribution.

The hostile concern is that this map mechanically creates the reversal. The available nested evidence only partially supports a defense:

- **B-T uses the same policy map** and yields `Delta_M^(B-T)<0`. Therefore the map by itself, without product repositioning, does not mechanically make SU preferred.
- **B-X at zero depth** also yields `Delta_M^(B-X)<0`, but Stage 11R correctly shows that this cannot identify policy endogeneity as necessary because it changes the policy level.
- **B-EQ fixes depth at the FULL equilibrium value and leaves locations endogenous; it reproduces FULL.** This pins the observed reversal on the post-policy positioning response at the chosen positive depth, not on the endogeneity of depth.
- A `v=0` no-network disappearance test is **not established in the current verification chain**. Stage 6R2 therefore does not use “the mechanism vanishes without network effects” as a novelty defense.

Conclusion: the same-map B-T comparison rejects the strongest “purely mechanical map” criticism, but the result remains conditional on the stylized policy map and positive-depth environment. The defensible description is a strategic-response result under this architecture, not a general theorem for arbitrary interoperability maps.

## 11. Surviving residual and permitted novelty language

### Surviving residual

> **Policy-induced strategic re-differentiation coalition-stability reversal.** There exists a regular parameter region in which a regional harmonization policy that is not preferred to international standardization when product positions are held fixed becomes nationally preferred once firms are allowed to reposition after the same standards architecture, because member re-differentiation raises domestic producer rents enough to overturn the IS–SU welfare ranking and stabilize the regional standards union.

This is narrower than the pre-Stage-11R `two endogenous margins are jointly necessary` claim.

### Permitted manuscript language

> Existing work separately contains government standards coalitions, continuous compatibility policy, endogenous product characteristics after standards policy, and partial interoperability. The contribution here is a narrower equilibrium feedback: under the modeled regional harmonization architecture, allowing post-policy horizontal repositioning can reverse member governments' IS–SU welfare ranking relative to the fixed-position evaluation and thereby change coalition stability.

### Prohibited manuscript language

Do not write:

- “first model/paper to study ...”;
- “novel framework combining ...”;
- “both endogenous harmonization depth and endogenous product location are necessary”;
- “neither margin alone can generate SU, only their interaction can”;
- “coalition-level harmonization depth is novel”;
- “regional standardization can be stable is novel”;
- “strategic response to interoperability is novel.”

## 12. Limitations of the novelty verdict

1. Absence of a located theorem is not proof of absence from the literature.
2. Some older journal PDFs were publisher-restricted; model/result judgments were cross-checked against publisher full text/abstracts, institutional working papers, author-uploaded copies, or RePEc records where possible.
3. The 2026 interoperability frontier is moving quickly; Huang et al. is a working paper and may change.
4. The canonical witness is a constructive/numerical result, not a global analytic theorem over all parameters.
5. The policy map remains stylized; no claim of arbitrary-map robustness is authorized.
6. No `v=0` disappearance result was established at this gate.
7. Stage 11R's `B-EQ` identification result remains binding.

## 13. Final hostile-referee answer (<=150 words)

> Gandal–Shy supplies the three-country standards-union game; Ruiz supplies the timing from government recognition to endogenous product characteristics and prices. What neither supplies, nor what follows mechanically from combining their propositions, is the coalition-ranking effect used here: holding the standards architecture fixed, allowing firms to reposition on a separate horizontal dimension can raise regional members’ producer rents enough to overturn their national IS–SU welfare ranking and stabilize the regional union. Indeed, Ruiz reports that endogenizing product characteristics preserves his qualitative policy equilibrium, while Gandal–Shy has no post-policy product-location response. Our contribution is therefore not the architecture or any of these ingredients, but this specific feedback from policy-induced strategic re-differentiation to government coalition stability. We do not claim that policy endogeneity itself is necessary; at the canonical witness, fixing depth at its FULL equilibrium value reproduces FULL.
