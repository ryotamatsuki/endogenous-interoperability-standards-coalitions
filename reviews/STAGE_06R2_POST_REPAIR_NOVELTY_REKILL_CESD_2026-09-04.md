## Hostile referee reconstruction: “Gandal–Shy + Ruiz”

### 1. What comes directly from Gandal–Shy

Gandal & Shy provide the three-country international-standardization architecture: governments choose recognition/union structures; network effects and conversion costs matter; national welfare drives policy; a two-country standardization union can be attractive against the third country; and coalition/participation incentives determine whether international recognition or a regional union is sustainable.

### 2. What comes directly from Ruiz

Ruiz adds exactly the dangerous missing downstream architecture. Government standard-recognition policy is chosen first; firms then choose product characteristics in the endogenous-location extension; prices follow; governments value domestic consumer surplus plus domestic-firm profits. Ruiz also shows that mutual recognition induces excessive product differentiation because firms reposition to soften price competition.

### 3. Why this does not automatically generate C-ESD’s surviving result

Ruiz’s own comparison is decisive. When product characteristics become endogenous, he reports **similar qualitative government-policy results**: governments still reject the foreign standard. Endogenous differentiation changes welfare decomposition and can make the free-trade/mutual-recognition allocation excessively differentiated, but it does not produce a reversal of the government-policy equilibrium relative to the fixed-location benchmark. Thus the proposition needed for C-ESD — policy-induced repositioning overturning an IS-versus-SU national-welfare ranking — is not already contained in Ruiz.

Likewise, Gandal–Shy has the coalition game but no post-policy endogenous product repositioning stage. Appending Ruiz’s location stage is therefore natural but not mechanically sufficient: one must solve a new continuation game in which union-specific harmonization changes both within-bloc and cross-bloc competitive geometry, calculate induced relocation, then feed producer-rent and consumer-surplus effects back into national coalition incentives.

### 4. Is the C-ESD sign reversal an obvious corollary?

**No, not from the audited propositions.** The direction is ambiguous ex ante. Ruiz itself is evidence that adding endogenous differentiation can leave the policy equilibrium qualitatively unchanged. Gandal–Shy is evidence that SU/IS ranking depends on conversion costs and network effects but does not characterize the additional strategic-location feedback. The C-ESD reversal therefore requires a nontrivial equilibrium feedback rather than a notation substitution.

### 5. But the old “two endogenous margins are jointly necessary” claim still fails

This is an internal identification failure, not a prior-art absorption. Stage 11R’s `B-EQ` shows that at the witness the policy-depth choice hits the cap under both IS and SU, so making the depth endogenous is not necessary for the reversal. The central causal comparison is therefore fixed positions versus post-policy endogenous positions under the same effective standards architecture.

## Paper-by-paper hostile analysis

### Gandal & Shy (2001) — strongest coalition predecessor

**Classification: STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE.** It already has three countries, standards unions, network effects, conversion/compatibility costs, national welfare and SU-versus-international-standardization incentives. This permanently kills any claim that regional standards unions, coalition stability, or government standardization incentives are new. It does not contain a post-policy endogenous product-location stage and therefore does not establish the surviving repositioning-driven ranking reversal.

### Ruiz (2004) — strongest downstream-mechanism predecessor

**Classification: STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE.** It already has government standards policy -> product characteristics -> price competition -> national welfare, with domestic producer rents and endogenous excessive differentiation. This permanently kills any claim that standards policy can alter product differentiation or that producer rents can dominate consumer losses. The key non-absorption fact is that Ruiz says endogenous product characteristics leave the qualitative government-policy result robust; no standards-union stability reversal is derived.

### Klimenko (2009)

**Classification: STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE.** Continuous compatibility policy, network externalities, strategic governments and international agreements are present. This kills intensive government compatibility-policy novelty. The audited sources do not reveal endogenous post-policy horizontal repositioning or a standards-union participation reversal generated by that response.

### Economides & Flyer; Economides & Skrzypacz

These papers make coalition/compatibility architecture particularly dangerous. Firms can affiliate with standards coalitions and balance larger network benefits against stronger within-coalition competition. Economides & Skrzypacz also explicitly allow a degree of technical compatibility. But the coalition is privately chosen by firms, not a national-welfare standards union, and product characteristics do not reposition after policy. **STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE / COMPONENT OVERLAP**, not absorption.

### Baake & Boom

Endogenous inherent quality, network effects, compatibility and price competition are jointly present. The important distinction is strategic order: quality is chosen before compatibility/adaptor provision. There is no national standards-union stability problem. **COMPONENT OVERLAP.**

### Jonard & Schenk

Circular product differentiation, compatibility, network externalities and price competition are present. Again, no government coalition game and no C-ESD benchmark reversal. **COMPONENT OVERLAP.**

### Huang, Tan, Teh & Zhou (2026)

Weighted interoperability networks, platform competition, prices and welfare are at the 2026 frontier. Their abstract reports industry-wide full interoperability as both total-welfare- and platform-optimal. The paper therefore sharply limits any claim about interoperability-network design but does not, in the audited record, contain national coalition formation or post-policy product repositioning. **COMPONENT OVERLAP.**

### Kretschmer, Rasch, Shekhar & Wenzel (2025)

Mandated interoperability induces a strategic downstream response in a network market, making any generic “firms strategically react to interoperability mandates” claim untenable. The response is privacy/data strategy rather than spatial product repositioning, and there is no international standards-union stability comparison. **COMPONENT OVERLAP.**

## Stage-4R coalition-level depth repair

The repaired action set — only non-singleton blocs choose an additional coalition-internal harmonization depth — has close analogues in standards-coalition and compatibility-investment literatures. In particular, firm standards coalitions with coalition-specific compatibility and international compatibility-policy agreements are structurally close. Therefore the repaired instrument itself is **not** novel and must never carry the contribution.

The repair does not make the model a mere spatial relabeling of those papers, because the audited coalition models do not add a post-policy horizontal repositioning stage that feeds back through **national** welfare and changes a government coalition-stability ranking. But the repair substantially strengthens the obligation to cite coalition-compatibility literature prominently.

## Result-built-into-assumption attack

The pairwise map `tau_12=t_bar-s`, `tau_13=tau_23=t_bar+s/2` clearly creates a force toward within-union convergence and cross-union separation. That alone is insufficient to establish the headline mechanism: the fixed-position B-T benchmark uses the same policy map yet does not generate the SU member-welfare reversal. The zero-depth endogenous-position benchmark B-X0 likewise does not reverse the ranking. Hence the sign change is not mechanically equivalent to writing down the friction map.

However, Stage 11R prevents the stronger claim that *policy endogeneity itself* is a necessary independent channel. Because `B-EQ=FULL` at the witness, the essential strategic channel identified by the current evidence is **post-policy repositioning at the relevant harmonization depth**, not the mathematical endogeneity of the depth choice.

The mechanism should therefore be described as a policy-induced continuation response: harmonization changes relative competitive distances; union members re-differentiate in the horizontal product dimension; the resulting producer-rent gain can outweigh the member consumer loss and overturn the national IS/SU ranking.

## Strongest kill attempts and outcomes

1. **Direct Gandal–Shy absorption:** fails because there is no endogenous post-policy product differentiation.
2. **Direct Ruiz absorption:** fails because there is no three-country standards-union coalition-stability problem and Ruiz’s endogenous-location extension preserves the qualitative policy result rather than reversing it.
3. **Gandal–Shy + Ruiz immediate-corollary attack:** strongest threat, but not enough for absorption; the required coalition-specific relocation/welfare feedback is not a quoted or automatic proposition.
4. **Klimenko continuous-policy absorption:** fails on product repositioning and coalition stability.
5. **2024–2026 mandated-interoperability absorption:** strategic responses are now known, but no audited paper reaches policy -> strategic differentiation -> international coalition-participation ranking reversal.
6. **Coalition-investment / standards-alliance relabeling:** close on the policy instrument, but not on the national-welfare continuation feedback.
7. **Assumption-driven reversal:** weakened by B-T using the same friction map without the reversal; nevertheless robustness to alternative normalizations remains a model-generality issue, not a novelty result.

## Surviving residual

The residual is not “two endogenous margins interact.” It is:

> There exists a parameter region in which a standards architecture evaluated at inherited product positions favors international standardization, while allowing firms to strategically reposition after that policy changes member producer rents enough to reverse the national IS/SU ranking and stabilize a regional standards union, despite member consumer losses and lower global welfare than under international standardization.

This is narrower than the pre-Stage-11R claim and should be the only main novelty language authorized by this audit.

## IJIO hostile-referee answer (<=150 words)

Gandal and Shy (2001) provide the international standards-union architecture, while Ruiz (2004) shows that standard-recognition policy can affect firms’ subsequent product-location incentives. Our result is not the mere conjunction of those ingredients. Ruiz’s endogenous-location extension leaves his qualitative government-policy equilibrium unchanged; it does not generate a standards-union participation reversal. In our game, union-specific harmonization changes both within-union and cross-union competitive distances, inducing member firms to re-differentiate in a separate horizontal product dimension. The resulting producer-rent increase can outweigh the member consumer loss and reverse the national-welfare ranking between international standardization and a regional union relative to the fixed-position benchmark. We therefore claim only this ranking reversal generated by the post-policy strategic response, not novelty of standards unions, endogenous differentiation, compatibility policy, or strategic responses to interoperability regulation themselves.
