## 3. Proposition-by-proposition re-kill

### P-A — standards depth creates an interoperability/competition trade-off

**KILL as novelty.**

Economides–Skrzypacz already frames standards-coalition affiliation as a network-benefit versus intra-platform-competition trade-off. Klimenko models continuous strategic government compatibility policy. Contemporary interoperability work parameterizes interoperability strength/configuration and price/welfare effects. Menegaki–Serfes 2026 makes coordination depth itself a coalition-stability margin.

### P-B — compatibility chosen before product design causes stronger differentiation

**KILL as novelty.**

Woeckener (1999) directly establishes the relevant compatibility-before-location logic. Ruiz (2004) also contains government standards recognition with endogenous product characteristics. Gabszewicz–Marini–Tarola provides coalition-first endogenous quality/price architecture outside the standards setting.

### P-C — coalition/interoperability membership changes a later endogenous choice

**KILL as novelty.**

Guo–Liu–Nault (2024) has interoperability coalition participation followed by endogenous cooperative resource investment and equilibrium coalition size. The author's SSDI has continuous standardization followed by endogenous innovation-portfolio adjustment.

### P-D — standards/coordination breadth or depth changes the stable coalition

**KILL as novelty.**

Takarada (2020) covers regional versus multilateral standards stability. Economides–Skrzypacz covers network strength and equilibrium standards-coalition structures. Menegaki–Serfes (2026) explicitly studies breadth, depth, and coalition stability; their current voluntary-standards working-paper listing further closes off any broad claim about stable interoperability-standard participation or privately versus socially preferred depth.

### P-E — costly post-policy repositioning shifts the optimized government coalition blocking threshold relative to fixed positions

**SURVIVES.**

This is not contained in the located close papers and is not an immediate corollary of one of them. It requires the full feedback:

`government coalition -> optimized standards depth -> post-policy product repositioning -> Bertrand competition -> national welfare -> strict coalition blocking`,

and the contribution is identified by comparing that full game to the otherwise identical fixed-position policy game.

## 4. Strongest close papers reopened

### Woeckener (1999)

Very close on the downstream mechanism: compatibility before product design can induce strong differentiation and soften competition. It does not contain a multilateral government standards-depth game or a national-welfare coalition blocking threshold. It therefore kills setup/mechanism novelty but does not absorb P-E.

### Economides & Skrzypacz (2003/04)

Very close on standards coalitions and the compatibility/network-benefit versus competition trade-off. Coalition affiliation is the upstream object and downstream competition follows. There is no separate costly product-positioning stage whose removal produces the fixed-position threshold benchmark used in P-E.

### Klimenko (2009)

Very close on continuous strategic government compatibility policy and network effects. It does not contain the three-country coalition partition/blocking problem with costly post-policy repositioning.

### Takarada (2020)

Very close on government standards harmonization and coalition/core stability. It lacks the endogenous product-positioning continuation that creates the certified B-FIX/FULL threshold difference.

### Guo, Liu & Nault (2024)

Very close on interoperability coalition formation followed by an endogenous resource decision and coalition-size equilibrium. Its strategic margin is resource investment in critical IT infrastructure rather than product-market repositioning under Bertrand competition; it does not generate P-E.

### Huang, Tan, Teh & Zhou (2026)

Very close on interoperability strength/configuration and equilibrium pricing/welfare. The available paper treats an interoperability network rather than endogenous government coalition blocking with costly product repositioning.

### Menegaki & Serfes (2026)

This is the most important new Stage-6 threat. *Coalition Stability with Complementary Goods* allows governance mechanisms such as compatibility standards/APIs to stabilize coordination. *Breadth, Depth, and Coalition Stability in Complementary Oligopoly* directly uses breadth/depth as coordination dimensions and derives stability changes. The current author webpage also lists *The Architecture of Voluntary Standards in Complementary Oligopoly*, summarized as a voluntary interoperability-standard participation problem with stable adoption and private/social depth differences.

These papers kill any claim that standards depth, participation architecture, or depth-dependent coalition stability is new. Based on the available public descriptions, however, they do not contain costly post-standard product repositioning or the P-E fixed-position-versus-FULL blocking-threshold result.

### Own prior work

`standardization-scope-direction-innovation` prevents a claim based on continuous standardization -> endogenous firm strategy -> policy ranking. `private-compatibility-standards-coalitions` prevents a claim based on formal standards partitions, private response, national welfare, and coalition stability alone.

P-E survives because its downstream margin and result are different: costly product positioning changes a coalition blocking threshold after endogenous depth optimization.
