## 2. Exact consumer-surplus identity

On each Salop arc `ij`, with boundary `y_ij`, exact consumer surplus is

`CS_ij=(-p_i+v n_i)y_ij-tau_ij y_ij^2/2`

`      +(-p_j+v n_j)(ell_ij-y_ij)-tau_ij(ell_ij-y_ij)^2/2`.

Summing arcs gives exact aggregate `CS`. No triangular approximation is used.

Because market share equals the mass of consumers assigned to each firm,

`CS = A + v q'G_rho q - sum_i p_i q_i - TC`,

where `TC` is total standard-induced Salop transportation/adaptation cost and `A` is the common gross-utility constant.

Firm profit is

`Pi_i=p_i q_i-C_i^D`,

with `C_i^D=gamma d_c(x_i,h_i)^2/2` in the baseline.

Therefore world welfare satisfies the exact transfer-cancellation identity

`GW = CS + sum_i Pi_i`

`   = A + v q'G_rho q - TC - sum_i C_i^D`.

Price payments are transfers globally and cancel exactly.

## 3. National welfare and coalition condition

National welfare remains

`W_i=CS/3+Pi_i`.

For a prospective SU member define

`Delta_M = W_M^SU-W^IS`.

Then exactly

`Delta_M = Delta Pi_M + Delta CS/3`.

Therefore the member joins / supports SU iff

`Delta Pi_M > -Delta CS/3`.

This is the clean economic threshold generated by Stage 7.

At the witness:

`Delta CS/3=-0.0325785`,

`Delta Pi_M=+0.0341498`,

so the inequality holds narrowly but strictly.

This is a rent-shifting / strategic-differentiation wedge, not a consumer-surplus gain.

## 6. Generality beyond quadratic redesign cost

The quadratic cost is useful for closed-form location FOCs but is not the conceptual source of the mechanism.

Let member outward displacement be `d>=0` and replace the baseline cost locally by a differentiable convex cost `C(d)` with

`C(0)=C'(0)=0`, `C''(d)>0`.

Let `MB_SU(d;s,v)` denote the member's marginal operating-profit gain from further outward repositioning under an SU policy profile.

The private location condition becomes

`MB_SU(d;s,v)=C'(d)`.

The Stage-3 / Stage-4 SU asymmetry result implies `MB_SU(0;s,v)>0` in the selected regular region, while the corresponding symmetric IS/SW location force is zero at inherited symmetric positions. Therefore any regular strictly convex cost with finite local curvature generates positive SU re-differentiation.

The magnitude falls with adjustment-cost curvature. Hence the coalition reversal has a natural intermediate-curvature structure:

1. if curvature is too high, re-differentiation is too weak and `Delta_M<0`;
2. at intermediate curvature, the profit gain exceeds the per-country CS loss and `Delta_M>0`;
3. if curvature is too low, local stationary points may cease to be global best responses because firms can jump across cyclic-order regions.

Thus the quadratic specification pins down a convenient one-parameter representation of a broader economic condition; it does not manufacture the sign reversal by itself.

## 9. Institutional validation

Primary-source audit is recorded in `literature/STAGE7_CESD_INSTITUTIONAL_VALIDATION.md`.

Two genuinely different settings survive as **institutional analogues**, not direct empirical proofs of the model:

1. EV charging infrastructure — EU AFIR uses common technical specifications explicitly for interoperability of charging connectors. This strongly validates the policy-controlled interface-friction primitive.
2. Digital messaging — EU DMA Article 7 requires designated gatekeepers to provide technical interfaces enabling interoperability of basic messaging functionalities. This validates a continuous/technical interoperability-policy margin in a non-physical network service.

In both settings, the policy/interoperability primitive is established. The specific C-ESD prediction that firms respond by increasing differentiation on other product dimensions remains a testable implication, not an established fact.

## 10. Generality audit

The mechanism does not require a specific industry label. It requires four economic objects:

1. a policy-controlled compatibility/interface dimension;
2. network value or installed-base reach that differs by interoperability regime;
3. a distinct horizontal product-characteristic margin firms can reposition on;
4. national/government objectives that include domestic producer rents but not foreign profits.

These objects can occur in both physical infrastructure standards and digital interoperability mandates.

Generality does **not** extend to settings where interoperability exhausts all product differentiation, where firms cannot reposition on any other dimension, or where a single global planner internalizes all producer rents.

## 11. Empirical predictions

The frozen model implies the following discriminating predictions:

1. **Within-bloc interface convergence with non-interface divergence:** deeper regional standard integration should reduce interface incompatibility while increasing differentiation on other product characteristics among member firms.
2. **Regime asymmetry:** the repositioning response should be stronger under partial/regional interoperability than under symmetric industry-wide interoperability.
3. **Adjustment-cost heterogeneity:** firms with lower but not negligible redesign/repositioning costs should respond more strongly; very high adjustment costs suppress the effect.
4. **Rent/consumer divergence:** regions where SU becomes attractive to member governments should show producer-rent gains alongside weaker consumer-surplus performance relative to IS.
5. **Outsider loss:** the excluded firm's/country's welfare should fall when member firms exploit the new differentiation geometry.
6. **Organizational misallocation:** regional interoperability can be politically stable even when global welfare favors industry-wide interoperability.

## 12. Policy scope and limits

The model does not imply that regional standards unions are generally bad, nor that full interoperability is always optimal.

The Stage-7 welfare result is conditional: in the identified region, national governments over-weight domestic producer rents relative to global welfare because foreign profit is excluded from national objectives and strategic repositioning redistributes rents.

No policy recommendation involving subsidies, transfers, antitrust remedies, side payments or mandatory product-design constraints is authorized because those instruments are absent from the model.

## 13. Remaining major concerns

1. The exact lower global-BR threshold is not available in closed form because cyclic-order changes create non-smooth global deviations.
2. The national-CS split is deliberately symmetric (`CS/3`). Country-specific consumer incidence is a later robustness question, not part of the frozen baseline.
3. Institutional evidence establishes interoperability policy instruments, not observed strategic re-differentiation. The empirical mapping is therefore suggestive rather than proven.
4. The strongest novelty attack remains Ruiz (2004) + Gandal–Shy (2001); publication value depends on presenting the welfare/rent-capture threshold rather than setup novelty.

None of these is a Stage-7 fatal blocker.
