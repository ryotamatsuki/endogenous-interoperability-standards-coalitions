Working title: **Standards Coalitions and Strategic Product Repositioning**

Frozen contribution:

> Allowing firms to reposition after a standards policy can reverse the member-country welfare ranking between international standardization and a regional standards union relative to a fixed-position evaluation of the same standards architecture, making the regional union stable through producer-rent gains even though member consumers lose and world welfare remains higher under international standardization.

Stage 11R2 final status: **GO TO JOURNAL POSITIONING**, supported by a pre-specified robustness grid with `valid_points=9/9` and `reversal_points=9/9`.

## 1. Executive positioning recommendation

The paper is best positioned as an **industrial-organization theory paper with an international standards-policy application**, not as a trade-theory paper whose core contribution is trade policy and not as a digital-platform paper whose mechanism depends on information markets.

The strongest current primary target is **International Journal of Industrial Organization (IJIO)**. Its scope explicitly covers theoretical industrial organization, strategic behavior, market structure, regulation and technological change, and it has recently published theory directly on interoperability and network effects. The paper's mechanism — standards policy changing competitive distances, followed by endogenous horizontal repositioning and price competition — is native to the journal's audience.

Recommended ladder:

1. **Stretch:** The Journal of Industrial Economics (JIE)
2. **Primary:** International Journal of Industrial Organization (IJIO)
3. **Realistic fallback:** Review of Industrial Organization (RIO)
4. **Safety net:** Journal of Industry, Competition and Trade (JICT)

Information Economics and Policy (IEP) was considered but rejected from the main ladder: it clearly welcomes theoretical policy research, but its current scope is centered on information, telecommunications, media, innovation/IP and information technology. The present model is broader standards/IO and would require an unnecessary digital-information framing to look fully native.

Official source:
`https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14676451/homepage/productinformation.html`

This remains a defensible stretch because the mechanism is clean and policy-relevant, but it should not displace IJIO as the expected-value primary target.

### Review of Industrial Organization — REALISTIC FALLBACK

Current Springer content shows the journal continues to publish compact formal IO theory. In 2024 it published **“Location, Location, Quality: The Fixed Differentiation Principle”**, explicitly revisiting a location-then-price spatial competition model; in 2025 it published additional formal oligopoly work.

### Journal of Industry, Competition and Trade — SAFETY NET

The journal regularly publishes work at the intersection of industrial organization, competition policy, industrial policy and trade. Recent 2026 content includes Duso and Peitz on aligning competition and industrial policy in the EU, demonstrating active interest in competition-policy interactions with international/industrial policy.

Recent article:
`https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10842-026-00470-3`

Desk/referee risk: **low-to-medium** relative to the candidates above. The topic is within reach, although the paper's formal IO mechanism may be more technical than necessary for part of its audience. It is a credible safety net without changing the theory.

### Information Economics and Policy — CONSIDERED, NOT IN MAIN LADDER

Official scope centers policy-oriented research on production, distribution and use of information, including telecommunications, media, innovation/IP and information technology; it welcomes both theoretical and empirical work.

Official source:
`https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/information-economics-and-policy/0167-6245`

Recent theory on multimarket platforms and cross-market externalities confirms openness to network/externality models. However, the present standards-coalition model is not inherently an information-industry model. Choosing IEP would create pressure to over-specialize the interpretation to digital standards. That violates the Stage-12 rule that journal choice must follow the paper rather than force a new substantive framing.

## 4. Primary target and rationale

**PRIMARY: International Journal of Industrial Organization.**

Reasoning:

1. The paper is fundamentally an IO game: policy changes effective competitive distances; firms reposition; prices and profits adjust; national welfare determines coalition stability.
2. IJIO explicitly welcomes theoretical work on strategic behavior, market structure, regulation and technological change.
3. Recent IJIO publication on interoperability and network effects verifies current audience fit.
4. The surviving novelty is result-level rather than ingredient-level, which is acceptable for IJIO if the mechanism and welfare consequence are sharply explained.
5. Stage 11R2's 9/9 pre-specified robustness directly addresses the most obvious numerical-witness desk/referee concern.
6. No substantive model extension is required to fit IJIO.

## 5. Stretch / fallback / safety logic

### Stretch — JIE

Use only if deliberately choosing upside over expected probability. Submission requires especially sharp policy implications and careful explanation that the contribution is the coalition-ranking consequence of strategic repositioning, not merely another Hotelling variant.

### Fallback — RIO

If IJIO rejects for insufficient breadth/novelty rather than a fatal mathematical issue, RIO is the preferred next outlet. Do not add a new mechanism before fallback. Reposition the introduction toward spatial/product-differentiation theory plus standards policy.

### Safety — JICT

Use after an RIO rejection if the core theory remains intact. Emphasize regional-versus-international standards coordination, national versus world welfare, and strategic industry response.
